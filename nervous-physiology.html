🧠 Nervous System Physiology

High-yield notes about neurons, action potentials, synapses, reflexes and autonomic nervous system.

1. Introduction

The nervous system receives sensory information, processes information and produces appropriate responses through electrical and chemical signals.

Basic sequence: Stimulus → Sensory input → Integration → Motor response

2. Neuron

The neuron is the basic functional cell of the nervous system.

Main Parts

  • Cell body (soma)
  • Dendrites
  • Axon
  • Axon terminals
Dendrites generally receive signals, while the axon conducts signals away from the cell body.

3. Neuroglia

Neuroglial cells support and protect neurons.

Cell Main Function
Astrocytes Support neurons and help maintain the extracellular environment
Oligodendrocytes Form myelin in the CNS
Schwann cells Form myelin in the PNS
Microglia Immune defense in the CNS

4. Resting Membrane Potential

Resting membrane potential is the electrical potential difference across the membrane of a resting neuron.

Typical neuronal resting membrane potential ≈ −70 mV

Important Factors

  • Unequal distribution of ions
  • Selective membrane permeability
  • Potassium leak channels
  • Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase

5. Action Potential

An action potential is a rapid, temporary change in membrane potential that travels along an excitable cell.

Main Phases

  1. Resting state
  2. Depolarization
  3. Repolarization
  4. Hyperpolarization
Depolarization: Voltage-gated sodium channels open and Na⁺ enters the cell.
Repolarization: Sodium channels become inactive and potassium efflux contributes to restoration of membrane potential.

6. Threshold and All-or-None Principle

An action potential is generated when the membrane reaches threshold.

All-or-none principle: Once threshold is reached, the action potential occurs with a relatively fixed amplitude.

7. Refractory Period

The refractory period is the period during which another action potential is difficult or impossible to generate.

Types

  • Absolute refractory period: another action potential cannot be initiated.
  • Relative refractory period: a stronger-than-normal stimulus may initiate another action potential.

8. Myelin and Saltatory Conduction

Myelin electrically insulates the axon and increases the speed of nerve impulse conduction.

Nodes of Ranvier

Nodes of Ranvier are gaps between myelinated segments of an axon.

Saltatory conduction: The action potential effectively jumps from node to node.

9. Synapse

A synapse is a functional junction through which information passes from one neuron to another neuron or an effector cell.

Types

  • Chemical synapse
  • Electrical synapse
Chemical synapses commonly use neurotransmitters to transmit signals.

10. Important Neurotransmitters

Neurotransmitter Important Role
Acetylcholine Neuromuscular transmission and autonomic functions
Glutamate Major excitatory neurotransmitter in the CNS
GABA Major inhibitory neurotransmitter in the CNS
Dopamine Movement, motivation and reward pathways
Serotonin Mood, sleep and other functions
Norepinephrine Attention and sympathetic nervous system functions

11. Synaptic Transmission

  1. Action potential reaches presynaptic terminal.
  2. Voltage-gated calcium channels open.
  3. Ca²⁺ enters the presynaptic terminal.
  4. Neurotransmitter is released.
  5. Neurotransmitter binds to receptors.
  6. Postsynaptic response is produced.

12. Sensory Physiology

Sensory receptors detect changes in the internal or external environment.

Examples

  • Touch
  • Pain
  • Temperature
  • Pressure
  • Vibration
  • Proprioception

13. Pain Sensation

Pain is a protective sensory experience associated with actual or potential tissue damage.

Nociceptors

Nociceptors are sensory receptors that respond to potentially damaging stimuli.

Pain pathways involve transmission of signals from peripheral receptors to the central nervous system.

14. Reflex Arc

A reflex is a rapid, automatic response to a stimulus.

Components of Reflex Arc

  1. Receptor
  2. Sensory neuron
  3. Integration center
  4. Motor neuron
  5. Effector
Example: Withdrawal reflex helps protect the body from harmful stimuli.

15. Autonomic Nervous System

The autonomic nervous system regulates many involuntary functions of the body.

Division General Function
Sympathetic Supports "fight or flight" responses
Parasympathetic Supports "rest and digest" functions

16. Sympathetic Nervous System

The sympathetic system prepares the body for increased activity and stress.

  • Increases heart rate
  • Dilates pupils
  • Promotes bronchodilation
  • Reduces gastrointestinal activity
  • Promotes blood flow to skeletal muscles

17. Parasympathetic Nervous System

The parasympathetic system generally promotes maintenance and digestion.

  • Decreases heart rate
  • Promotes gastrointestinal activity
  • Promotes glandular secretions
  • Facilitates energy conservation

18. Central Nervous System

The central nervous system consists of the brain and spinal cord.

Main Functions

  • Processing sensory information
  • Integration of information
  • Control of motor responses
  • Higher cognitive functions

19. Major Brain Functions

Region Important Functions
Cerebrum Higher cognitive functions, sensation and voluntary movement
Cerebellum Coordination, balance and motor learning
Brainstem Important pathways and vital autonomic functions
Hypothalamus Homeostasis, endocrine and autonomic regulation

20. Spinal Cord

The spinal cord connects the brain with peripheral nerves and also contains important reflex circuits.

  • Transmits sensory information to the brain.
  • Carries motor commands from the brain.
  • Coordinates many reflexes.

⭐ Important Exam Points

  • Basic functional cell of nervous system = Neuron
  • Typical resting membrane potential ≈ −70 mV
  • Depolarization is mainly associated with Na⁺ entry.
  • Repolarization is mainly associated with K⁺ movement out of the cell.
  • Myelin increases conduction velocity.
  • Schwann cells form myelin in the PNS.
  • Oligodendrocytes form myelin in the CNS.
  • GABA is a major inhibitory neurotransmitter in the CNS.
  • Glutamate is a major excitatory neurotransmitter in the CNS.
  • Reflex arc includes receptor, sensory neuron, integration center, motor neuron and effector.
  • Sympathetic system is associated with fight-or-flight responses.
  • Parasympathetic system is associated with rest-and-digest functions.
