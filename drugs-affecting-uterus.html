1. Introduction to Drugs Affecting Uterus

Drugs affecting the uterus are important in obstetric and gynecological practice. They can increase uterine contractions, reduce postpartum bleeding, induce or augment labour, facilitate uterine evacuation, or decrease uterine contractions when premature labour needs to be delayed.

Major Groups:
  • Uterotonics: Oxytocin and prostaglandins
  • Tocolytics: Drugs that reduce uterine contractions
  • Uterine relaxants: Mainly used when reduction of uterine activity is required

2. Oxytocin

Oxytocin is a naturally occurring peptide hormone produced in the hypothalamus and released from the posterior pituitary. It is one of the most important uterotonic drugs.

Mechanism of Action

Oxytocin binds to oxytocin receptors on uterine smooth muscle. These receptors are Gq-coupled and increase intracellular calcium, producing rhythmic uterine contractions.

Oxytocin → Oxytocin receptor → Gq → IP3/DAG → ↑ Ca²⁺ → Uterine contraction

Major Effects

  • Increases frequency and strength of uterine contractions.
  • Promotes cervical dilatation indirectly by producing contractions.
  • Causes milk ejection by contraction of myoepithelial cells.

Clinical Uses

  • Induction of labour when medically indicated.
  • Augmentation of labour when uterine contractions are inadequate.
  • Prevention and treatment of postpartum hemorrhage due to uterine atony.
  • Facilitation of uterine contraction after delivery.

Adverse Effects

  • Uterine tachysystole/hyperstimulation.
  • Uterine rupture in susceptible patients, especially with excessive stimulation.
  • Fetal distress secondary to excessive uterine activity.
  • Hypotension, especially with rapid IV administration.
  • Water intoxication and hyponatremia with prolonged high-dose infusion.

Important Precautions

  • Should be administered under appropriate obstetric monitoring when used for labour induction or augmentation.
  • Excessive uterine contractions can compromise fetal oxygenation.
  • Rapid IV bolus administration should be avoided.
High-Yield Point: Oxytocin is a major first-line uterotonic for prevention and treatment of postpartum hemorrhage caused by uterine atony.

3. Prostaglandins

Prostaglandins are lipid mediators that can produce cervical ripening, uterine contractions and changes in smooth muscle activity. Several prostaglandin analogues are used in obstetric practice.

Important Prostaglandin Drugs

Drug Main Uses Important Point
Dinoprostone (PGE₂) Cervical ripening and induction of labour Promotes cervical softening and uterine activity
Misoprostol (PGE₁ analogue) Cervical ripening, induction of labour, medical abortion and prevention of NSAID-induced gastric ulcers Causes uterine contractions
Carboprost (15-methyl PGF₂α) Postpartum hemorrhage due to uterine atony Powerful uterotonic; bronchospasm is important

Mechanism of Action

Prostaglandins act on specific prostaglandin receptors in the uterus and cervix. Depending on the drug and receptor, they can increase uterine smooth-muscle activity and promote cervical ripening.

Dinoprostone

  • Prostaglandin E₂ analogue.
  • Used mainly for cervical ripening.
  • Can also assist induction of labour.
  • May cause uterine tachysystole, fever, nausea or vomiting.

Misoprostol

  • Prostaglandin E₁ analogue.
  • Produces cervical ripening and uterine contractions.
  • Used in selected obstetric indications according to clinical protocols.
  • Also prevents NSAID-associated gastric ulcers.
  • Common adverse effects include abdominal cramping, diarrhea, nausea and fever.
Important: Misoprostol causes uterine contractions and should not be used during an ongoing desired pregnancy for routine ulcer prevention.

Carboprost

  • 15-methyl analogue of PGF₂α.
  • Strong uterotonic.
  • Used for postpartum hemorrhage due to uterine atony when appropriate.
  • Important adverse effect: bronchospasm.
  • Generally avoided in patients with significant asthma.

Common Adverse Effects of Prostaglandins

  • Uterine hyperstimulation.
  • Abdominal cramps.
  • Nausea and vomiting.
  • Diarrhea.
  • Fever and chills.
  • Bronchospasm, particularly with carboprost.

4. Tocolytic Drugs

Tocolytics are drugs that decrease uterine contractions. They may be used in selected patients with threatened preterm birth to temporarily delay delivery, often to allow time for interventions such as antenatal corticosteroids or transfer to an appropriate facility.

Important Tocolytics

Drug/Class Mechanism Important Adverse Effects
Nifedipine Calcium-channel blocker; reduces calcium entry into smooth muscle Hypotension, headache, flushing, dizziness
Terbutaline β₂ agonist; increases cAMP and relaxes uterine smooth muscle Tachycardia, tremor, hyperglycemia, hypokalemia
Indomethacin COX inhibitor → decreases prostaglandin synthesis GI effects; fetal renal effects and premature ductus arteriosus constriction with later/prolonged exposure
Magnesium sulfate Reduces neuromuscular excitability; not a routine first-line tocolytic Flushing, weakness, respiratory depression in toxicity

Nifedipine

Nifedipine is a dihydropyridine calcium-channel blocker. By reducing calcium entry into smooth muscle cells, it decreases myometrial contraction.

  • Commonly used as a tocolytic in many clinical settings.
  • Can cause maternal hypotension.
  • Headache, flushing and dizziness may occur.

β₂ Agonists

Drugs such as terbutaline stimulate β₂ receptors and increase intracellular cAMP, producing smooth-muscle relaxation.

  • Decrease uterine contractions.
  • May cause maternal tachycardia.
  • Tremor and palpitations are common.
  • Hypokalemia and hyperglycemia may occur.

Indomethacin

Indomethacin inhibits cyclooxygenase and reduces prostaglandin synthesis, thereby decreasing uterine activity.

High-Yield Point: NSAID use for tocolysis is generally limited by gestational-age considerations because fetal renal dysfunction and premature ductus arteriosus constriction are important risks, especially with later pregnancy exposure.

Magnesium Sulfate

Magnesium sulfate is important in obstetrics, particularly for seizure prevention/treatment in pre-eclampsia/eclampsia and for fetal neuroprotection in selected very preterm births. It is not considered a routine first-line tocolytic.

Monitoring During Tocolytic Therapy

  • Maternal blood pressure and pulse.
  • Uterine activity.
  • Fetal condition when appropriate.
  • Drug-specific adverse effects.

5. Uterotonics vs Tocolytics

Feature Uterotonics Tocolytics
Main effect Increase uterine contraction Decrease uterine contraction
Examples Oxytocin, misoprostol, dinoprostone, carboprost Nifedipine, selected β₂ agonists, indomethacin
Main obstetric role Induction/augmentation of labour and control of uterine atony/PPH Temporary delay of preterm labour in selected patients

6. MBBS-Level Important Questions & Answers

Q1. What is oxytocin?

Answer: Oxytocin is a peptide hormone and uterotonic drug that stimulates uterine smooth-muscle contraction and is widely used for induction or augmentation of labour and prevention/treatment of postpartum hemorrhage due to uterine atony.

Q2. What is the mechanism of action of oxytocin?

Answer: Oxytocin activates Gq-coupled oxytocin receptors, increasing IP3, intracellular calcium and uterine smooth-muscle contraction.

Q3. Mention the important clinical uses of oxytocin.

Answer: Induction of labour, augmentation of labour and prevention/treatment of postpartum hemorrhage caused by uterine atony.

Q4. What is the major danger of excessive oxytocin administration?

Answer: Excessive uterine stimulation or tachysystole, which can compromise fetal oxygenation and, in susceptible patients, contribute to uterine rupture.

Q5. What are prostaglandins used for in obstetrics?

Answer: They are used for cervical ripening, induction of labour and, depending on the agent, management of postpartum hemorrhage and medical termination of pregnancy according to appropriate protocols.

Q6. Name two prostaglandin drugs affecting the uterus.

Answer: Dinoprostone and misoprostol. Carboprost is another important uterotonic prostaglandin analogue.

Q7. What is misoprostol?

Answer: Misoprostol is a prostaglandin E₁ analogue that promotes cervical ripening and uterine contractions. It is also used to prevent NSAID-associated gastric ulcers.

Q8. What is the important adverse effect of carboprost?

Answer: Bronchospasm is an important adverse effect; therefore carboprost is generally avoided in patients with significant asthma.

Q9. What are tocolytics?

Answer: Tocolytics are drugs that decrease uterine contractions and may temporarily delay preterm birth in selected patients.

Q10. Name important tocolytic drugs.

Answer: Nifedipine, selected β₂ agonists such as terbutaline, and indomethacin are important examples.

Q11. How does nifedipine act as a tocolytic?

Answer: Nifedipine blocks L-type calcium channels and reduces calcium entry into uterine smooth muscle, thereby decreasing contraction.

Q12. How do β₂ agonists relax the uterus?

Answer: β₂ receptor stimulation increases cAMP in smooth muscle, leading to relaxation of the myometrium.

Q13. Why is indomethacin useful as a tocolytic?

Answer: It inhibits COX enzymes and reduces prostaglandin synthesis, thereby decreasing uterine contractions.

Q14. What is an important fetal concern with indomethacin?

Answer: Fetal renal effects and premature constriction of the ductus arteriosus are important concerns, particularly with later or prolonged pregnancy exposure.

Q15. Differentiate uterotonics and tocolytics.

Answer: Uterotonics increase uterine contraction and are used for labour induction/augmentation and uterine atony. Tocolytics decrease uterine contraction and may temporarily delay selected preterm births.
