🫁 Thorax Anatomy

High-yield thorax anatomy notes for medical students.

📖 1. Introduction

The thorax is the region of the body between the neck and abdomen. It contains important organs and structures involved mainly in respiration and circulation.

The thorax contains the lungs, heart and major portions of the respiratory and cardiovascular systems.

🦴 2. Thoracic Wall

The thoracic wall protects the organs within the thoracic cavity and assists in respiration.

Main Components

  • Thoracic vertebrae
  • Ribs
  • Sternum
  • Intercostal muscles
  • Thoracic fascia and connective tissues

Important Structures

Structure Important Point
Sternum Anterior part of the thoracic skeleton.
Ribs Form much of the lateral thoracic wall.
Intercostal muscles Contribute to movements of the thoracic wall during breathing.

🫁 3. Thoracic Cavity

The thoracic cavity is enclosed by the thoracic wall and is separated from the abdominal cavity by the diaphragm.

Main Divisions

  • Right pleural cavity
  • Left pleural cavity
  • Mediastinum

The two pleural cavities contain the lungs, while the mediastinum contains the heart and many other important structures.

❤️ 4. Mediastinum

The mediastinum is the central compartment of the thoracic cavity between the two pleural cavities.

Main Divisions

Division Major Contents
Superior mediastinum Major vessels, trachea, esophagus and other structures.
Anterior mediastinum Small compartment anterior to the pericardium.
Middle mediastinum Contains the heart and pericardium.
Posterior mediastinum Contains the esophagus, thoracic aorta and other structures.

🫀 5. Heart

The heart is a muscular organ located in the middle mediastinum. It functions as the main pump of the cardiovascular system.

Heart Chambers

  • Right atrium
  • Right ventricle
  • Left atrium
  • Left ventricle

Major Valves

Valve Location
Tricuspid valve Between right atrium and right ventricle.
Pulmonary valve Between right ventricle and pulmonary trunk.
Mitral valve Between left atrium and left ventricle.
Aortic valve Between left ventricle and aorta.

🫁 6. Lungs

The lungs are the major organs of respiration and are located within the right and left pleural cavities.

Right Lung

  • Three lobes
  • Superior lobe
  • Middle lobe
  • Inferior lobe

Left Lung

  • Two lobes
  • Superior lobe
  • Inferior lobe
  • Cardiac notch
Lung Number of Lobes
Right lung 3
Left lung 2

🫁 7. Pleura

The pleura is a serous membrane associated with the lungs.

Two Layers

  • Visceral pleura — covers the surface of the lung.
  • Parietal pleura — lines the thoracic cavity.

The pleural cavity lies between the visceral and parietal pleura.

🩸 8. Major Blood Vessels

The thorax contains several major vessels of the systemic and pulmonary circulations.

  • Aorta
  • Pulmonary trunk
  • Superior vena cava
  • Pulmonary veins

Basic Circulation

Right ventricle → Pulmonary trunk → Lungs → Pulmonary veins → Left atrium

🫁 9. Diaphragm

The diaphragm is a dome-shaped skeletal muscle that separates the thoracic cavity from the abdominal cavity and plays a major role in respiration.

Important Openings

Structure Important Association
Caval opening Transmits the inferior vena cava.
Esophageal hiatus Transmits the esophagus.
Aortic hiatus Transmits the aorta.

⭐ Important Exam Points

  • The thoracic cavity contains two pleural cavities and the mediastinum.
  • The right lung has three lobes.
  • The left lung has two lobes and a cardiac notch.
  • The heart is located in the middle mediastinum.
  • Visceral pleura covers the lung surface.
  • Parietal pleura lines the thoracic cavity.
  • The diaphragm separates the thoracic and abdominal cavities.
  • Know the four chambers and four major heart valves.

⚡ Quick Revision

  • Thorax → Thoracic wall + two pleural cavities + mediastinum
  • Right lung → 3 lobes
  • Left lung → 2 lobes + cardiac notch
  • Heart → Right atrium, right ventricle, left atrium, left ventricle
  • Heart valves → Tricuspid, Pulmonary, Mitral, Aortic
  • Pleura → Visceral + Parietal
  • Diaphragm → Separates thorax from abdomen
