Gastrointestinal Pharmacology

MedAcademy - Medical Students

1. Introduction to Gastrointestinal Pharmacology

Gastrointestinal pharmacology deals with drugs that affect the stomach, intestines, liver and related gastrointestinal functions. These drugs are used to treat acid-related disorders, nausea and vomiting, constipation, diarrhea, inflammatory bowel disease and other GI conditions.

Major GI Drug Groups:
  • Drugs for peptic ulcer disease and GERD
  • Antacids
  • H2-receptor blockers
  • Proton pump inhibitors
  • Mucosal protective agents
  • Antiemetic drugs
  • Prokinetic drugs
  • Laxatives
  • Antidiarrheal drugs
  • Drugs for inflammatory bowel disease
  • Drugs for irritable bowel syndrome
  • Drugs affecting biliary function
  • Digestive enzyme preparations

2. Important GI Physiological Targets

Target Function / Importance
Proton Pump H+/K+-ATPase Final pathway for gastric acid secretion
H2 Receptor Stimulates gastric acid secretion through histamine
Muscarinic M3 Receptor Promotes gastric acid secretion and GI smooth muscle activity
5-HT3 Receptor Important in nausea and vomiting pathways
D2 Receptor Dopamine can inhibit GI motility; blocking D2 can promote gastric emptying
Mu Opioid Receptor Decreases intestinal motility and secretion

3. Peptic Ulcer Disease

Peptic ulcer disease occurs when the protective mechanisms of the gastrointestinal mucosa are overwhelmed by damaging factors such as gastric acid, pepsin, Helicobacter pylori infection and NSAID use.

Important Causes

  • Helicobacter pylori infection
  • NSAID use
  • Excess acid production in selected conditions
  • Smoking and other risk factors

Major Treatment Principles

  • Acid suppression
  • H. pylori eradication when present
  • Stopping or reducing offending NSAIDs when possible
  • Mucosal protection in selected patients
High-Yield: H. pylori treatment generally requires combination antimicrobial therapy plus acid suppression. The exact regimen depends on local resistance patterns and current guidelines.

4. Antacids

Antacids are weak bases that neutralize gastric hydrochloric acid. They provide rapid symptomatic relief but do not suppress acid secretion.

Common Antacids

  • Aluminium hydroxide
  • Magnesium hydroxide
  • Calcium carbonate
  • Sodium bicarbonate

Aluminium Hydroxide

Aluminium salts can cause constipation and, with excessive or prolonged use, may contribute to phosphate depletion.

Magnesium Hydroxide

Magnesium salts can cause diarrhea and may accumulate in significant renal impairment.

Classic Comparison:

Aluminium → Constipation
Magnesium → Diarrhea

Calcium Carbonate

Calcium carbonate can cause constipation and, with excessive intake, hypercalcemia and other complications.

Sodium Bicarbonate

It reacts rapidly with acid but is not preferred for routine long-term self-treatment because of systemic sodium and acid-base effects.

5. H2-Receptor Blockers

H2-receptor blockers reduce gastric acid secretion by blocking histamine H2 receptors on gastric parietal cells.

Examples

  • Famotidine
  • Cimetidine
  • Nizatidine

Mechanism

H2 receptor blockade → ↓ cAMP in parietal cells → ↓ H+/K+-ATPase activity → ↓ Gastric acid secretion

Uses

  • GERD
  • Peptic ulcer disease
  • Nocturnal acid-related symptoms

Cimetidine

Cimetidine inhibits several CYP450 enzymes and therefore has more clinically important drug interactions than many other H2 blockers.

High-Yield: Famotidine generally has fewer clinically important CYP-mediated drug interactions than cimetidine.

6. Proton Pump Inhibitors (PPIs)

PPIs are among the most powerful suppressors of gastric acid secretion. They irreversibly inhibit the gastric H+/K+-ATPase proton pump in parietal cells.

Examples

  • Omeprazole
  • Esomeprazole
  • Pantoprazole
  • Lansoprazole
  • Rabeprazole

Mechanism

PPI → activation in acidic canaliculi → irreversible inhibition of H+/K+-ATPase → profound suppression of gastric acid secretion

Uses

  • GERD
  • Peptic ulcer disease
  • H. pylori eradication regimens
  • Prevention of selected NSAID-related ulcers
  • Zollinger-Ellison syndrome

Adverse Effects / Long-Term Considerations

  • Headache
  • Diarrhea or gastrointestinal symptoms
  • Possible increased risk of certain enteric infections
  • Hypomagnesemia with prolonged use in susceptible patients
  • Vitamin B12 deficiency with prolonged therapy in some patients
  • Possible increase in fracture risk in selected high-risk patients
Exam Point: PPIs inhibit the final common pathway of gastric acid secretion.

7. Mucosal Protective Drugs

Sucralfate

Sucralfate forms a protective barrier over ulcerated mucosa in an acidic environment.

Sucralfate can interfere with absorption of several oral medicines. It may also cause constipation.

Bismuth Compounds

Bismuth compounds have protective and antimicrobial activity and can be used as part of certain H. pylori eradication regimens.

Bismuth-containing quadruple therapy commonly includes a PPI, bismuth, tetracycline and metronidazole, depending on current guidelines and local resistance patterns.

Misoprostol

Misoprostol is a prostaglandin E1 analogue that decreases gastric acid secretion and increases mucosal protective mechanisms.

Important: Misoprostol can cause uterine contractions and is contraindicated during pregnancy when used for prevention of NSAID-induced ulcers.

8. Drugs for GERD

Gastroesophageal reflux disease occurs when gastric contents reflux into the esophagus and cause symptoms or mucosal injury.

Drug Options

  • Antacids - rapid symptom relief
  • H2 blockers - acid suppression
  • PPIs - stronger acid suppression
  • Alginate-containing preparations - physical barrier in selected products
General Principle: PPIs are commonly preferred for frequent or erosive GERD because they provide stronger acid suppression than H2 blockers.

9. Antiemetic Drugs

Antiemetic drugs prevent or reduce nausea and vomiting by acting on different receptors in the vomiting pathways.

Drug/Class Main Receptor/Action Important Uses
Ondansetron 5-HT3 antagonist Chemotherapy-related nausea, postoperative nausea
Metoclopramide D2 antagonist; also promotes GI motility Nausea, selected gastroparesis/GERD situations
Domperidone Peripheral D2 antagonist Selected nausea and motility disorders
Prochlorperazine D2 antagonist Nausea and vomiting
Promethazine H1 antagonist with anticholinergic effects Nausea, motion sickness
Scopolamine Muscarinic antagonist Motion sickness
Aprepitant NK1 antagonist Chemotherapy-induced nausea and vomiting

10. Ondansetron

Ondansetron selectively blocks 5-HT3 receptors and is particularly useful for chemotherapy-induced nausea and vomiting and postoperative nausea.

Adverse Effects

  • Headache
  • Constipation
  • QT interval prolongation risk
High-Yield: Ondansetron → 5-HT3 receptor antagonist.

11. Metoclopramide

Metoclopramide is a dopamine D2 receptor antagonist that also enhances upper GI motility and accelerates gastric emptying.

Uses

  • Nausea and vomiting
  • Selected cases of gastroparesis
  • Selected reflux-related conditions

Important Adverse Effects

  • Acute dystonia
  • Akathisia
  • Other extrapyramidal symptoms
  • Tardive dyskinesia with prolonged exposure
  • Hyperprolactinemia
Important: Metoclopramide crosses the blood-brain barrier, so extrapyramidal adverse effects are clinically important.

12. Prokinetic Drugs

Prokinetic drugs increase gastrointestinal motility or improve gastric emptying.

Examples

  • Metoclopramide
  • Domperidone
  • Prucalopride
  • Selected macrolide therapy such as erythromycin for short-term gastric motility

Domperidone

Domperidone is a peripheral D2 receptor antagonist and has less CNS penetration than metoclopramide.

Domperidone can prolong the QT interval and increase arrhythmia risk in susceptible patients.

13. Laxatives

Laxatives are used to treat constipation and facilitate bowel evacuation. They can be divided into several classes.

Class Examples Main Action
Bulk-forming Psyllium Increases stool bulk and water content
Osmotic Lactulose, PEG, Magnesium salts Retain water in intestinal lumen
Stimulant Bisacodyl, Senna Stimulate intestinal motility/secretion
Stool softener Docusate Facilitates water mixing with stool
Secretagogues Lubiprostone, Linaclotide Increase intestinal fluid secretion

14. Bulk-Forming Laxatives

Psyllium

Psyllium is a soluble fiber that absorbs water and increases stool bulk. It is useful for chronic constipation and can also help regulate stool consistency.

Adequate fluid intake is important when using bulk-forming agents to reduce the risk of obstruction.

15. Osmotic Laxatives

Lactulose

Lactulose is a nonabsorbable disaccharide that is metabolized by colonic bacteria, increasing osmotic water retention in the bowel.

Important Clinical Use: Lactulose is also used to reduce intestinal ammonia absorption in hepatic encephalopathy.

Polyethylene Glycol (PEG)

PEG retains water in the intestinal lumen and is widely used for constipation and bowel preparation.

Magnesium Salts

Magnesium hydroxide and related salts draw water into the bowel and can produce relatively rapid bowel movements.

Magnesium-containing laxatives require caution in significant renal impairment because magnesium can accumulate.

16. Stimulant Laxatives

Bisacodyl

Bisacodyl stimulates intestinal motility and secretion and is used for short-term treatment of constipation and bowel preparation.

Senna

Senna contains stimulant compounds that increase colonic motility.

Possible adverse effects include abdominal cramping and diarrhea.

17. Antidiarrheal Drugs

Loperamide

Loperamide is a peripheral opioid receptor agonist that decreases intestinal motility and increases transit time.

Important: Loperamide should not be used to suppress diarrhea when invasive bacterial infection is suspected, particularly when there is high fever or bloody stool, without appropriate medical assessment.

Bismuth Subsalicylate

Bismuth subsalicylate has antisecretory and antimicrobial effects and may provide symptomatic relief in some diarrheal illnesses.

Oral Rehydration Solution

Oral rehydration solution is a cornerstone of treatment for many cases of acute diarrhea because sodium-glucose cotransport allows effective intestinal water absorption.

Most Important Principle: In acute diarrhea, prevention and treatment of dehydration is more important than simply stopping bowel movements.

18. Drugs for Inflammatory Bowel Disease

Inflammatory bowel disease includes ulcerative colitis and Crohn's disease. Drug selection depends on disease location, severity and treatment goals.

5-Aminosalicylates

  • Mesalamine
  • Sulfasalazine

5-ASA drugs have anti-inflammatory effects and are particularly important in ulcerative colitis.

Corticosteroids

  • Prednisone
  • Budesonide

Corticosteroids are used mainly for induction of remission rather than long-term maintenance because of their adverse-effect profile.

Immunomodulators

  • Azathioprine
  • 6-Mercaptopurine
  • Methotrexate

Biologic Therapies

  • Infliximab
  • Adalimumab
  • Vedolizumab
  • Ustekinumab
Treatment of moderate-to-severe IBD often requires specialist assessment and monitoring for infection and other drug-specific complications.

19. Drugs for Irritable Bowel Syndrome

Drug treatment of IBS is based on the dominant symptoms and may include therapies for constipation, diarrhea or abdominal pain.

IBS with Constipation

  • Fiber
  • Polyethylene glycol for constipation symptoms
  • Linaclotide
  • Lubiprostone in selected patients

IBS with Diarrhea

  • Loperamide for diarrhea control
  • Selected prescription therapies according to clinical assessment

Abdominal Cramping

Antispasmodic agents may be used in selected patients to reduce intestinal smooth muscle spasm and abdominal discomfort.

20. Drugs Affecting Biliary Function

Ursodeoxycholic Acid

Ursodeoxycholic acid is a hydrophilic bile acid used in selected cholestatic liver diseases and certain cholesterol gallstones.

Chenodeoxycholic Acid

This bile acid has limited modern clinical use but is historically important in dissolution therapy for selected cholesterol stones.

21. Digestive Enzyme Preparations

Pancreatic Enzyme Replacement Therapy

Pancreatic enzyme preparations contain lipase, amylase and proteases. They are used in pancreatic exocrine insufficiency.

Example: Pancrelipase is commonly used in pancreatic exocrine insufficiency associated with conditions such as chronic pancreatitis or cystic fibrosis.

22. H. pylori Eradication

Helicobacter pylori infection is an important cause of peptic ulcer disease and is associated with increased risk of gastric cancer and gastric MALT lymphoma.

Common Drug Components

  • Proton pump inhibitor
  • Amoxicillin
  • Clarithromycin in selected settings
  • Metronidazole
  • Tetracycline
  • Bismuth
Important: The exact eradication regimen should follow current local resistance patterns and guideline recommendations. Clarithromycin resistance can significantly affect regimen selection.

23. Important GI Drug Interactions

Drug Important Interaction / Concern
Cimetidine Inhibits several CYP450 enzymes and can increase concentrations of some drugs
Antacids Can reduce absorption of certain medicines by changing gastric pH or binding drugs
Sucralfate Can reduce absorption of several oral medicines
PPIs Can alter absorption of drugs requiring acidic gastric conditions
Metoclopramide May alter GI absorption of some orally administered drugs because of increased gastric emptying

25. High-Yield Exam Points

  • PPIs are the most potent commonly used suppressors of gastric acid secretion.
  • PPIs inhibit the gastric H+/K+-ATPase proton pump.
  • H2 blockers inhibit histamine H2 receptors on parietal cells.
  • Famotidine generally has fewer CYP-mediated interactions than cimetidine.
  • Antacids neutralize existing gastric acid rather than stopping its production.
  • Aluminium salts commonly cause constipation.
  • Magnesium salts commonly cause diarrhea.
  • Sucralfate forms a protective barrier over ulcerated mucosa.
  • Misoprostol is useful for prevention of selected NSAID-induced ulcers.
  • Misoprostol can cause uterine contractions and should not be used during pregnancy for ulcer prevention.
  • Ondansetron is a 5-HT3 receptor antagonist.
  • Metoclopramide blocks D2 receptors and has prokinetic effects.
  • Metoclopramide can cause extrapyramidal symptoms.
  • Loperamide is a peripheral opioid receptor agonist used for diarrhea.
  • Oral rehydration is essential in many cases of acute diarrhea.
  • Lactulose can be used for hepatic encephalopathy.
  • Mesalamine is especially important in ulcerative colitis.
  • PPIs are commonly used as part of H. pylori eradication therapy.
  • H. pylori treatment should account for local antibiotic resistance.
  • Pancreatic enzyme replacement is used in pancreatic exocrine insufficiency.

27. Gastrointestinal Pharmacology MCQs

1. What is the final common pathway for gastric acid secretion?

A. Na+/K+ ATPase

B. H+/K+-ATPase

C. Ca2+ ATPase

D. Na+/Ca2+ exchanger

Answer: B. H+/K+-ATPase
2. Which class produces the most potent routine suppression of gastric acid?

A. Antacids

B. H2 blockers

C. PPIs

D. Prokinetics

Answer: C. PPIs
3. Which H2 blocker is well known for CYP450 inhibition?

A. Famotidine

B. Cimetidine

C. Nizatidine

D. None

Answer: B. Cimetidine
4. Which drug is a 5-HT3 receptor antagonist?

A. Ondansetron

B. Metoclopramide

C. Loperamide

D. Lactulose

Answer: A. Ondansetron
5. Which antiemetic can cause extrapyramidal symptoms?

A. Ondansetron

B. Metoclopramide

C. Lactulose

D. Psyllium

Answer: B. Metoclopramide
6. Which antacid commonly causes constipation?

A. Magnesium hydroxide

B. Aluminium hydroxide

C. Magnesium citrate

D. Sodium bicarbonate

Answer: B. Aluminium hydroxide
7. Which drug is a peripheral opioid receptor agonist used for diarrhea?

A. Loperamide

B. Ondansetron

C. Omeprazole

D. Sucralfate

Answer: A. Loperamide
8. Which drug is used in hepatic encephalopathy?

A. Lactulose

B. Bisacodyl

C. Omeprazole

D. Loperamide

Answer: A. Lactulose
9. Which drug forms a protective barrier over ulcerated mucosa?

A. Sucralfate

B. Famotidine

C. Metoclopramide

D. Loperamide

Answer: A. Sucralfate
10. Which drug is a prostaglandin E1 analogue?

A. Misoprostol

B. Ondansetron

C. Mesalamine

D. Lactulose

Answer: A. Misoprostol
11. Which drug is particularly important in ulcerative colitis?

A. Mesalamine

B. Loperamide

C. Famotidine

D. Bisacodyl

Answer: A. Mesalamine
12. Which laxative is a bulk-forming agent?

A. Psyllium

B. Lactulose

C. Bisacodyl

D. Senna

Answer: A. Psyllium
13. Which drug class is commonly used for H. pylori eradication?

A. PPI-based combination therapy

B. Antacid alone

C. Loperamide alone

D. Psyllium alone

Answer: A. PPI-based combination therapy
14. Which drug is a proton pump inhibitor?

A. Omeprazole

B. Famotidine

C. Sucralfate

D. Ondansetron

15. Which drug is used for pancreatic exocrine insufficiency?

A. Pancrelipase
