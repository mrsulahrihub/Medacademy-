1. Introduction to Drug Receptors

Drug receptors are specialized cellular macromolecules, usually proteins, that recognize and bind drugs or endogenous signaling molecules and initiate a biological response.

Key Concept: Receptors are important because they help convert drug binding into a cellular response.

2. Functions of Receptors

  • Recognize specific chemical signals.
  • Bind drugs or endogenous ligands.
  • Convert ligand binding into a cellular signal.
  • Regulate physiological and biochemical processes.
  • Contribute to therapeutic and adverse drug effects.

3. Major Types of Drug Receptors

Pharmacologically important receptors can be divided into four major families.

Receptor Type Mechanism Typical Response Example
Ligand-gated ion channels Directly open or close ion channels Very rapid Nicotinic acetylcholine receptor
G-protein coupled receptors Activate G-proteins and second messengers Seconds to minutes β-adrenergic receptor
Enzyme-linked receptors Activate intracellular enzyme pathways Minutes to hours Insulin receptor
Intracellular receptors Alter gene transcription Slow and prolonged Glucocorticoid receptor

4. Ligand-Gated Ion Channels

Ligand-gated ion channels are membrane receptors that directly control the movement of ions across the cell membrane after binding of a ligand.

Characteristics

  • Located in the cell membrane.
  • Directly regulate ion flow.
  • Produce very rapid responses.
Example: Nicotinic acetylcholine receptors form ligand-gated ion channels.

5. G-Protein Coupled Receptors (GPCRs)

GPCRs are membrane receptors that transmit signals through heterotrimeric G-proteins.

Major G-Protein Pathways

  • Gs: Stimulates adenylyl cyclase and increases cAMP.
  • Gi: Inhibits adenylyl cyclase and decreases cAMP.
  • Gq: Activates phospholipase C and increases IP3 and DAG.
Exam Point: Gs → ↑ cAMP
Gi → ↓ cAMP
Gq → ↑ IP3 + DAG

6. Enzyme-Linked Receptors

Enzyme-linked receptors are membrane receptors associated with enzymatic activity or intracellular signaling enzymes.

Examples

  • Insulin receptor
  • Epidermal growth factor receptor
  • Cytokine receptors associated with JAK-STAT signaling
Example: The insulin receptor is a receptor tyrosine kinase.

7. Intracellular Receptors

Intracellular receptors are located inside the cell, either in the cytoplasm or nucleus.

Drugs that act through these receptors are generally lipid-soluble enough to cross the cell membrane.

Examples

  • Glucocorticoids
  • Mineralocorticoids
  • Thyroid hormones
  • Sex hormones
  • Vitamin D
Important: Intracellular receptor responses are generally slower because they often involve changes in gene transcription and protein synthesis.

8. Important Second Messengers

Second messengers are intracellular signaling molecules that transmit signals from activated receptors to intracellular targets.

  • cAMP
  • cGMP
  • IP3
  • DAG
  • Intracellular Ca²⁺

9. Receptor Affinity

Affinity is the ability of a drug to bind to its receptor.

Remember: High affinity means strong binding tendency, but it does not necessarily mean that the drug will produce a strong response.

10. Receptor Selectivity

Selectivity refers to the tendency of a drug to act more strongly on one receptor or target than on others at appropriate concentrations.

Important: Selectivity is often dose-dependent. As drug concentration increases, activity at additional targets may occur.

11. Receptor Upregulation and Downregulation

Upregulation

Upregulation is an increase in receptor number or responsiveness.

Downregulation

Downregulation is a decrease in receptor number or responsiveness.

Long-term exposure to an agonist can contribute to receptor desensitization and downregulation.

12. Receptor Desensitization

Desensitization means that a receptor or signaling system becomes less responsive despite continued exposure to an agonist.

  • May occur after repeated or prolonged stimulation.
  • Can involve receptor phosphorylation.
  • Can involve receptor internalization.
  • May contribute to tolerance.

13. Spare Receptors

Spare receptors are present when a maximal biological response can be produced without occupying all available receptors.

Concept: Spare receptors can increase the sensitivity of a tissue to an agonist.

14. Receptor Reserve

Receptor reserve describes the excess number of receptors available beyond those required to produce a maximal response.

It is closely related to the concept of spare receptors.

15. Quick Comparison of Receptor Types

Type Speed Main Mechanism
Ligand-gated ion channel Very fast Ion movement
GPCR Fast to moderate G-proteins / second messengers
Enzyme-linked receptor Moderate Enzyme / kinase signaling
Intracellular receptor Slow Gene transcription

16. Quick Revision Points

  • Receptors are usually proteins.
  • There are four major receptor families.
  • Ligand-gated ion channels produce very rapid responses.
  • GPCRs act through G-proteins and second messengers.
  • Gs increases cAMP.
  • Gi decreases cAMP.
  • Gq activates the IP3/DAG pathway.
  • Insulin receptor is a receptor tyrosine kinase.
  • Intracellular receptors commonly regulate gene transcription.
  • Affinity describes receptor binding.
  • Selectivity describes preferential action at particular targets.
  • Receptor desensitization reduces response to continued stimulation.

17. Important MCQs

Q1. Which receptor type produces the fastest response?

A. Intracellular receptor
B. Enzyme-linked receptor
C. Ligand-gated ion channel
D. GPCR

Answer: C. Ligand-gated ion channel
Q2. Which G-protein increases cAMP?

A. Gi
B. Gs
C. Gq
D. G12

Answer: B. Gs
Q3. Gq primarily activates:

A. Adenylyl cyclase
B. Guanylyl cyclase
C. Phospholipase C
D. Tyrosine kinase

Answer: C. Phospholipase C
Q4. Insulin receptor belongs to which class?

A. GPCR
B. Ligand-gated ion channel
C. Receptor tyrosine kinase
D. Intracellular receptor

Answer: C. Receptor tyrosine kinase
Q5. Which receptor type commonly acts by changing gene transcription?

A. Ligand-gated ion channel
B. GPCR
C. Intracellular receptor
D. Voltage-gated channel

Answer: C. Intracellular receptor
