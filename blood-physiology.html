🩸 Blood Physiology

Complete high-yield notes for medical students

1. Introduction to Blood

Blood is a specialized connective tissue that circulates through the cardiovascular system.

It transports oxygen, nutrients, hormones and metabolic waste products throughout the body.

Main components of blood:
  • Plasma
  • Red Blood Cells (RBCs)
  • White Blood Cells (WBCs)
  • Platelets

2. Plasma

Plasma is the liquid component of blood in which blood cells and other substances are suspended.

Main Components

  • Water
  • Plasma proteins
  • Electrolytes
  • Nutrients
  • Hormones
  • Metabolic waste products

Major Plasma Proteins

Protein Main Function
Albumin Maintains plasma oncotic pressure and transports substances
Globulins Transport and immune functions
Fibrinogen Important for blood clot formation

3. Red Blood Cells (RBCs)

Red blood cells, also called erythrocytes, are specialized cells responsible mainly for the transport of oxygen.

Characteristics

  • Biconcave disc-shaped cells
  • Mature RBCs lack a nucleus
  • Contain hemoglobin
  • Highly flexible
  • Designed for efficient gas transport

Functions of RBCs

  • Transport oxygen
  • Help transport carbon dioxide
  • Contribute to acid-base balance

4. Hemoglobin

Hemoglobin is an iron-containing protein present inside red blood cells.

Its major function is to bind and transport oxygen.

Important: One hemoglobin molecule can bind up to 4 oxygen molecules.

5. RBC Lifespan

The average lifespan of a red blood cell is approximately 120 days.

Old or damaged RBCs are mainly removed by macrophages, particularly in the spleen and liver.

Important RBC Facts

  • RBCs are produced in red bone marrow.
  • They contain hemoglobin.
  • Mature RBCs do not have a nucleus.
  • Average lifespan ≈ 120 days.

6. Erythropoiesis

Erythropoiesis is the process of formation of red blood cells.

In adults, erythropoiesis mainly occurs in the red bone marrow.

Erythropoietin

Erythropoietin (EPO) is the major hormone that stimulates RBC production.

The kidneys are the major source of erythropoietin in adults.

Low oxygen → increased EPO → increased RBC production

7. White Blood Cells (WBCs)

White blood cells, or leukocytes, are important components of the immune system.

Main Types of WBCs

WBC Main Function
Neutrophils Important in acute bacterial infections and phagocytosis
Lymphocytes Adaptive and immune responses
Monocytes Become macrophages in tissues and perform phagocytosis
Eosinophils Important in parasitic infections and allergic responses
Basophils Release mediators involved in inflammatory and allergic responses

8. WBC Classification

Granulocytes

  • Neutrophils
  • Eosinophils
  • Basophils

Agranulocytes

  • Lymphocytes
  • Monocytes
Mnemonic:
Granulocytes = Neutrophils + Eosinophils + Basophils

Agranulocytes = Lymphocytes + Monocytes

9. Platelets

Platelets, also called thrombocytes, are small cellular fragments derived from megakaryocytes in the bone marrow.

Functions

  • Help stop bleeding
  • Form the platelet plug
  • Participate in coagulation
  • Release substances involved in vessel repair

10. Hemostasis

Hemostasis is the physiological process that prevents excessive blood loss after vascular injury.

Main Steps of Hemostasis

  1. Vasoconstriction — narrowing of the injured blood vessel.
  2. Platelet Plug Formation — platelets adhere to the injured area and aggregate.
  3. Coagulation — formation of a fibrin clot stabilizes the platelet plug.

11. ABO Blood Group System

The ABO system classifies blood according to the presence or absence of A and B antigens on red blood cells.

Blood Group RBC Antigen Plasma Antibodies
A A Anti-B
B B Anti-A
AB A and B None against A/B
O None Anti-A and Anti-B

12. Rh Blood Group System

The Rh system is another important blood group system. The D antigen is the most clinically important Rh antigen.

  • Rh positive → D antigen is present.
  • Rh negative → D antigen is absent.
Clinical importance: Rh incompatibility can be important during pregnancy, particularly when an Rh-negative mother carries an Rh-positive fetus.

⭐ Important Exam Points

  • Average RBC lifespan = 120 days.
  • Main function of RBCs = oxygen transport.
  • Hemoglobin contains iron.
  • Main site of adult erythropoiesis = red bone marrow.
  • Major hormone stimulating RBC production = erythropoietin.
  • Major source of EPO in adults = kidneys.
  • Granulocytes = neutrophils, eosinophils, basophils.
  • Agranulocytes = lymphocytes, monocytes.
  • Platelets are important for hemostasis.
  • Hemostasis includes vasoconstriction, platelet plug formation and coagulation.
  • Rh-positive blood has the D antigen.
