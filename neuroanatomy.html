🧠 Neuroanatomy

Brain, spinal cord, meninges, ventricles and CSF

📚 Introduction to Neuroanatomy

Neuroanatomy is the study of the structure and organization of the nervous system.

Major divisions:

  • Central Nervous System (CNS)
  • Peripheral Nervous System (PNS)

Central Nervous System includes:

  • Brain
  • Spinal cord

🧠 Brain

The brain is the major organ of the central nervous system. It is located inside the cranial cavity.

Main divisions:

  • Cerebrum
  • Diencephalon
  • Brainstem
  • Cerebellum

🧠 Cerebrum

The cerebrum is the largest part of the brain and is responsible for many higher functions.

Two cerebral hemispheres:

  • Right cerebral hemisphere
  • Left cerebral hemisphere

Major lobes:

  • Frontal lobe
  • Parietal lobe
  • Temporal lobe
  • Occipital lobe
  • Insula

🎯 Major Functions of Cerebral Lobes

Frontal Lobe

  • Voluntary motor control
  • Planning and decision-making
  • Behavior and personality
  • Speech production through the dominant hemisphere's Broca area

Parietal Lobe

  • Processes somatic sensory information
  • Helps with spatial awareness

Temporal Lobe

  • Hearing
  • Memory
  • Language comprehension in the dominant hemisphere

Occipital Lobe

  • Mainly associated with visual processing

🌉 Brainstem

The brainstem connects the cerebrum with the spinal cord and contains important pathways and nuclei.

Three parts:

  1. Midbrain
  2. Pons
  3. Medulla oblongata

The brainstem is also important for maintaining vital functions such as respiration and cardiovascular control.

⚖️ Cerebellum

The cerebellum is located posterior to the brainstem. It plays an important role in coordination and balance.

Important functions:

  • Coordination of movement
  • Balance
  • Posture
  • Motor learning

🧬 Spinal Cord

The spinal cord is a long part of the central nervous system extending from the brainstem through the vertebral canal.

Main functions:

  • Carries sensory information toward the brain
  • Carries motor information from the brain
  • Acts as a center for many reflexes

🧠 Spinal Cord Segments

The spinal cord is divided into segments associated with pairs of spinal nerves.

  • 8 cervical segments
  • 12 thoracic segments
  • 5 lumbar segments
  • 5 sacral segments
  • 1 coccygeal segment

In adults, the spinal cord usually ends around the L1–L2 vertebral level as the conus medullaris.

🛡️ Meninges

The meninges are protective membranes surrounding the brain and spinal cord.

Three layers:

  1. Dura mater
  2. Arachnoid mater
  3. Pia mater

Easy order:

Dura → Arachnoid → Pia

💧 Cerebrospinal Fluid (CSF)

Cerebrospinal fluid surrounds the brain and spinal cord and circulates through the ventricular system and subarachnoid space.

Functions:

  • Provides mechanical protection
  • Provides buoyancy to the brain
  • Helps maintain a stable environment for the CNS

🕳️ Brain Ventricles

The ventricular system consists of interconnected cavities within the brain that contain CSF.

Main ventricles:

  • Right lateral ventricle
  • Left lateral ventricle
  • Third ventricle
  • Fourth ventricle

Important pathway:

Lateral ventricles → Third ventricle → Cerebral aqueduct → Fourth ventricle

🩸 Blood Supply of the Brain

The brain receives arterial blood mainly through the internal carotid and vertebral arteries.

Major arterial systems:

  • Internal carotid artery system
  • Vertebrobasilar system

These vessels contribute to the arterial circulation around the base of the brain.

🔄 Circle of Willis

The Circle of Willis is an arterial network located at the base of the brain that provides connections between major cerebral arteries.

Important components include:

  • Anterior cerebral arteries
  • Anterior communicating artery
  • Internal carotid arteries
  • Posterior communicating arteries
  • Posterior cerebral arteries

⚡ Neuron

A neuron is a specialized cell responsible for transmitting electrical and chemical signals.

Main parts:

  • Cell body (soma)
  • Dendrites
  • Axon

🔌 Synapse

A synapse is a specialized junction through which a neuron communicates with another neuron or target cell.

Common neurotransmitters include:

  • Acetylcholine
  • Dopamine
  • Serotonin
  • Glutamate
  • GABA

⭐ Important Exam Points

  • CNS consists of brain and spinal cord.
  • Cerebrum is the largest part of the brain.
  • Major cerebral lobes are frontal, parietal, temporal and occipital.
  • Brainstem consists of midbrain, pons and medulla.
  • Cerebellum is important for coordination and balance.
  • Meninges are dura mater, arachnoid mater and pia mater.
  • CSF circulates through the ventricular system and subarachnoid space.
  • There are two lateral ventricles, a third ventricle and a fourth ventricle.
  • Brain blood supply mainly comes from internal carotid and vertebral arteries.
  • Circle of Willis is located at the base of the brain.
  • The spinal cord usually ends around L1–L2 in adults.
  • A neuron consists of a cell body, dendrites and an axon.

⚡ Quick Revision

  • CNS: Brain + spinal cord
  • Cerebrum: Higher brain functions
  • Frontal lobe: Motor, planning and behavior
  • Parietal lobe: Somatic sensation
  • Temporal lobe: Hearing and memory
  • Occipital lobe: Vision
  • Brainstem: Midbrain + pons + medulla
  • Cerebellum: Coordination and balance
  • Meninges: Dura + arachnoid + pia
  • CSF: Protection and buoyancy
  • Ventricles: CSF-containing cavities
  • Neuron: Basic functional cell of nervous tissue
