Respiratory Pharmacology

MedAcademy - Medical Students

1. Introduction to Respiratory Pharmacology

Respiratory pharmacology deals with drugs used to prevent and treat diseases of the respiratory system. These drugs are commonly used in conditions such as asthma, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), allergic rhinitis, cough, and respiratory infections.

The major goals of respiratory pharmacotherapy are:

  • Relieve bronchoconstriction
  • Reduce airway inflammation
  • Improve airflow
  • Reduce mucus production
  • Control allergic symptoms
  • Suppress troublesome cough when appropriate
  • Prevent acute respiratory exacerbations
High-Yield: The two major pharmacological approaches in asthma are bronchodilation and anti-inflammatory therapy.

2. Important Respiratory Drug Targets

  • β2-adrenergic receptors
  • Muscarinic receptors
  • Glucocorticoid receptors
  • Leukotriene receptors
  • Histamine H1 receptors
  • Phosphodiesterase enzymes
  • Mast cells and inflammatory cells
  • Airway mucus glands

3. Bronchodilator Drugs

Bronchodilators relax airway smooth muscle and improve airflow. They are particularly important in asthma and COPD.

Main Bronchodilator Groups

  • β2-adrenergic agonists
  • Antimuscarinic drugs
  • Methylxanthines

4. β2-Adrenergic Agonists

β2 agonists stimulate β2 receptors in bronchial smooth muscle. Activation increases intracellular cAMP and causes relaxation of airway smooth muscle.

Mechanism

β2 receptor → Gs protein → ↑ Adenylyl cyclase → ↑ cAMP → Bronchial smooth muscle relaxation

Examples

  • Salbutamol (Albuterol)
  • Terbutaline
  • Formoterol
  • Salmeterol
  • Indacaterol
  • Vilanterol

Short-Acting β2 Agonists (SABA)

Examples include salbutamol and terbutaline. They produce relatively rapid bronchodilation.

Important: Salbutamol is commonly used as a rapid-relief bronchodilator for acute bronchospasm.

Long-Acting β2 Agonists (LABA)

Examples include salmeterol and formoterol. They provide prolonged bronchodilation.

LABAs should generally not be used alone for asthma control. In asthma, they are used with an inhaled corticosteroid.

Adverse Effects

  • Tremor
  • Palpitations
  • Tachycardia
  • Headache
  • Hypokalemia
  • Muscle cramps

5. Antimuscarinic Drugs

Antimuscarinic drugs block muscarinic receptors in the airways. This reduces parasympathetic bronchoconstriction and decreases mucus secretion.

Examples

  • Ipratropium
  • Tiotropium
  • Umeclidinium
  • Glycopyrronium

Mechanism

Muscarinic receptor blockade → ↓ bronchoconstriction → Bronchodilation

Ipratropium

Ipratropium is a short-acting muscarinic antagonist (SAMA). It is particularly useful in COPD and can also be used with a β2 agonist in acute asthma exacerbations.

Tiotropium

Tiotropium is a long-acting muscarinic antagonist (LAMA). It is an important maintenance bronchodilator in COPD and can be used as add-on therapy in selected patients with asthma.

Adverse Effects

  • Dry mouth
  • Blurred vision if drug reaches the eyes
  • Urinary retention
  • Constipation
High-Yield: Antimuscarinic drugs are especially important in COPD.

6. Methylxanthines

Methylxanthines are bronchodilators that inhibit phosphodiesterase and can increase intracellular cAMP.

Main Example

  • Theophylline

Actions

  • Bronchodilation
  • Stimulation of respiratory drive
  • Some anti-inflammatory effects

Adverse Effects

  • Nausea
  • Vomiting
  • Tremor
  • Insomnia
  • Tachycardia
  • Arrhythmias
  • Seizures in severe toxicity
Theophylline has a relatively narrow therapeutic range and many drug interactions, so toxicity is an important concern.

7. Corticosteroids in Respiratory Diseases

Corticosteroids are powerful anti-inflammatory drugs and are central to long-term control of asthma.

Inhaled Corticosteroids (ICS)

  • Budesonide
  • Beclomethasone
  • Fluticasone
  • Mometasone

Mechanism

Corticosteroids enter cells and bind intracellular glucocorticoid receptors. The drug-receptor complex affects gene transcription and reduces the production of many inflammatory mediators.

Effects

  • Reduce airway inflammation
  • Reduce airway hyperresponsiveness
  • Reduce mucosal edema
  • Reduce inflammatory cell activity

Adverse Effects of Inhaled Corticosteroids

  • Oral candidiasis
  • Dysphonia
  • Throat irritation
Rinsing the mouth after inhaled corticosteroid use can reduce the risk of oral candidiasis.

Systemic Corticosteroids

Examples include prednisone, prednisolone, and methylprednisolone. They may be used for severe asthma exacerbations and other inflammatory respiratory conditions.

8. Leukotriene Modifiers

Leukotrienes contribute to bronchoconstriction, airway edema and mucus production.

Examples

  • Montelukast
  • Zafirlukast
  • Zileuton

Montelukast and Zafirlukast

These drugs block leukotriene receptors, particularly CysLT1 receptors.

Zileuton

Zileuton inhibits 5-lipoxygenase and therefore reduces leukotriene synthesis.

Clinical Uses

  • Asthma
  • Exercise-induced bronchoconstriction
  • Allergic rhinitis
  • Selected patients with aspirin-exacerbated respiratory disease
Montelukast carries an important warning regarding possible neuropsychiatric adverse effects. Its use should be individualized.

9. Mast Cell Stabilizer

Cromolyn

Cromolyn reduces mast-cell mediator release and can help prevent allergen-induced bronchospasm.

It is mainly a preventive drug and is not used for rapid relief of acute bronchospasm.

10. Biologic Drugs for Severe Asthma

Biologic therapies target specific inflammatory pathways and are used in selected patients with severe asthma.

Examples

  • Omalizumab - anti-IgE
  • Mepolizumab - anti-IL-5
  • Reslizumab - anti-IL-5
  • Benralizumab - anti-IL-5 receptor
  • Dupilumab - blocks IL-4/IL-13 signaling
  • Tezepelumab - targets TSLP
Biologic therapy is phenotype/endotype-directed and is generally reserved for selected patients with severe asthma despite optimized standard treatment.

11. Pharmacological Management of Asthma

Asthma treatment aims to control symptoms, prevent exacerbations and maintain normal or near-normal lung function.

Reliever Therapy

Rapid bronchodilation may be provided by an inhaled short-acting bronchodilator. Current asthma strategies may also use an inhaled corticosteroid-containing reliever depending on the treatment plan.

Controller Therapy

  • Inhaled corticosteroids
  • ICS + LABA combinations
  • Leukotriene modifiers in selected patients
  • Long-acting muscarinic antagonists in selected patients
  • Biologic therapy for selected severe asthma
Key Point: Inhaled corticosteroids are the foundation of anti-inflammatory controller therapy in asthma.

12. Pharmacological Management of COPD

COPD treatment focuses on reducing symptoms, improving exercise tolerance and preventing exacerbations.

Common Drug Groups

  • Short-acting bronchodilators
  • Long-acting β2 agonists
  • Long-acting muscarinic antagonists
  • LABA + LAMA combinations
  • Inhaled corticosteroids in selected patients
  • PDE-4 inhibitor in selected patients

Roflumilast

Roflumilast is a phosphodiesterase-4 inhibitor. It reduces inflammation and is used in selected patients with severe COPD, particularly those with chronic bronchitis and frequent exacerbations.

Adverse Effects of Roflumilast

  • Diarrhea
  • Nausea
  • Weight loss
  • Headache
  • Insomnia

13. Antihistamines

Histamine contributes to allergic symptoms such as sneezing, itching and rhinorrhea.

First-Generation H1 Antihistamines

  • Diphenhydramine
  • Chlorpheniramine
  • Promethazine

They can cross the blood-brain barrier and commonly cause sedation.

Second-Generation H1 Antihistamines

  • Cetirizine
  • Loratadine
  • Fexofenadine

They are generally less sedating and are commonly used for allergic rhinitis.

14. Nasal Decongestants

Decongestants reduce nasal congestion by causing vasoconstriction of nasal mucosal blood vessels.

Examples

  • Pseudoephedrine
  • Phenylephrine
  • Oxymetazoline

Adverse Effects

  • Increased blood pressure
  • Palpitations
  • Insomnia
  • Anxiety
Topical nasal decongestants should generally not be used continuously for long periods because rebound nasal congestion can occur.

15. Drugs Used for Cough

Antitussives

Antitussives suppress the cough reflex and may be useful for selected troublesome dry coughs.

Examples

  • Dextromethorphan
  • Codeine

Dextromethorphan

Dextromethorphan is a centrally acting antitussive. It has less analgesic and respiratory-depressant activity than morphine, but excessive doses can still cause toxicity.

Codeine

Codeine is an opioid that can suppress cough but may cause sedation, constipation and respiratory depression.

16. Expectorants and Mucolytics

Guaifenesin

Guaifenesin is an expectorant that is intended to increase hydration of respiratory secretions and facilitate mucus clearance.

N-acetylcysteine (NAC)

N-acetylcysteine breaks disulfide bonds in mucus glycoproteins and can reduce mucus viscosity.

NAC is also an important antidote for acetaminophen poisoning, although that is a different clinical indication.

Hypertonic Saline

In selected airway diseases, inhaled hypertonic saline can help improve airway secretion clearance.

17. Respiratory Stimulants

Some drugs can stimulate respiratory drive, but their clinical roles are limited and condition-specific.

Doxapram

Doxapram is a respiratory stimulant that has limited modern clinical use. It is not a routine treatment for most respiratory failure.

20. Important Adverse Effects to Remember

  • β2 agonists: tremor, tachycardia, hypokalemia
  • Antimuscarinics: dry mouth, urinary retention, blurred vision
  • Theophylline: nausea, arrhythmias, seizures in severe toxicity
  • Inhaled corticosteroids: oral candidiasis, dysphonia
  • Systemic corticosteroids: hyperglycemia, osteoporosis, infection risk, adrenal suppression
  • Montelukast: possible neuropsychiatric adverse effects
  • First-generation antihistamines: sedation and anticholinergic effects
  • Decongestants: hypertension, palpitations, insomnia
  • Opioid antitussives: sedation and respiratory depression

21. Important Question Answers

Q1. What is the mechanism of β2 agonists?

β2 agonists activate Gs-coupled β2 receptors, increase cAMP and cause relaxation of bronchial smooth muscle.

Q2. What is the most important anti-inflammatory controller class in asthma?

Inhaled corticosteroids are the foundation of anti-inflammatory controller therapy for asthma.

Q3. Give examples of short-acting β2 agonists.

Salbutamol and terbutaline are examples of short-acting β2 agonists.

Q4. Give examples of long-acting β2 agonists.

Salmeterol and formoterol are examples of LABAs.

Q5. Why are antimuscarinic drugs useful in COPD?

They block muscarinic-mediated bronchoconstriction and reduce airway smooth-muscle contraction, producing bronchodilation.

Q6. What is the difference between SABA and LABA?

SABA provides relatively rapid, short-duration bronchodilation, whereas LABA provides prolonged bronchodilation for maintenance treatment.

Q7. Why should patients rinse their mouth after inhaled corticosteroids?

Rinsing reduces local steroid deposition and lowers the risk of oral candidiasis.

Q8. What is the mechanism of montelukast?

Montelukast blocks CysLT1 leukotriene receptors and reduces leukotriene-mediated bronchoconstriction and inflammation.

Q9. What is the major toxicity of theophylline?

Theophylline toxicity can cause nausea, vomiting, tachycardia, arrhythmias, tremor and seizures.

Q10. What is roflumilast?

Roflumilast is a PDE-4 inhibitor used in selected patients with severe COPD, especially those with chronic bronchitis and frequent exacerbations.

Q11. What is omalizumab?

Omalizumab is a monoclonal antibody against IgE used in selected patients with severe allergic asthma.

Q12. Why are LABAs generally combined with inhaled corticosteroids in asthma?

LABAs provide bronchodilation but do not adequately treat the underlying airway inflammation. Combining them with an inhaled corticosteroid improves control and avoids LABA monotherapy in asthma.

  • Inhaled corticosteroids are the foundation of anti-inflammatory asthma controller therapy.
  • Ipratropium is a short-acting muscarinic antagonist.
