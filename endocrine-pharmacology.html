Endocrine Pharmacology

MedAcademy - Medical Students

1. Introduction to Endocrine Pharmacology

Endocrine pharmacology deals with drugs that affect hormones, hormone receptors and endocrine organs. These medicines are used to replace deficient hormones, suppress excessive hormone production, or modify hormonal effects.

Major Endocrine Drug Groups:
  • Antidiabetic drugs
  • Thyroid and antithyroid drugs
  • Corticosteroids
  • Sex hormones
  • Contraceptive drugs
  • Drugs affecting pituitary hormones
  • Growth hormone drugs
  • Drugs affecting bone and calcium metabolism
  • Adrenal hormone drugs

2. Insulin

Insulin is an anabolic hormone that decreases blood glucose by increasing glucose uptake in skeletal muscle and adipose tissue and by reducing hepatic glucose production.

Types of Insulin

Type Examples General Feature
Rapid-acting Lispro, Aspart, Glulisine Rapid onset
Short-acting Regular insulin Can be given IV in selected emergencies
Intermediate-acting NPH Intermediate duration
Long-acting Glargine, Detemir Long duration
Ultra-long acting Degludec Very long duration

Mechanism

Insulin binds to a receptor tyrosine kinase and promotes glucose uptake, particularly through increased GLUT4 expression at the cell membrane in skeletal muscle and adipose tissue.

Major Adverse Effect: Hypoglycemia.

Other effects may include weight gain and, with some formulations, injection-site reactions.

3. Antidiabetic Drugs

Metformin

Metformin is commonly used as an initial drug for type 2 diabetes when appropriate. It primarily reduces hepatic glucose production and improves insulin sensitivity.

Important: Gastrointestinal upset is common. Rarely, metformin can cause lactic acidosis, with risk increased in certain severe renal or systemic conditions.

Sulfonylureas

  • Glibenclamide (Glyburide)
  • Glipizide
  • Gliclazide

Sulfonylureas stimulate pancreatic beta cells to release insulin.

Main adverse effects: Hypoglycemia and weight gain.

Meglitinides

  • Repaglinide
  • Nateglinide

These stimulate insulin release and have relatively short durations of action.

Thiazolidinediones

  • Pioglitazone

Pioglitazone activates PPAR-gamma and improves insulin sensitivity.

Possible adverse effects include weight gain, edema and increased risk of heart failure in susceptible patients.

DPP-4 Inhibitors

  • Sitagliptin
  • Linagliptin
  • Saxagliptin

They inhibit DPP-4 and increase endogenous incretin activity.

GLP-1 Receptor Agonists

  • Semaglutide
  • Liraglutide
  • Dulaglutide

GLP-1 receptor agonists increase glucose-dependent insulin secretion, reduce glucagon secretion, slow gastric emptying and promote satiety.

SGLT2 Inhibitors

  • Empagliflozin
  • Dapagliflozin
  • Canagliflozin

SGLT2 inhibitors reduce renal glucose reabsorption and increase urinary glucose excretion.

Important Clinical Point: SGLT2 inhibitors can provide cardiovascular and renal benefits in selected patients, in addition to glucose lowering.

4. Antidiabetic Drug Comparison

Drug/Class Main Action Important Adverse Effect
Insulin Increases glucose uptake and decreases glucose production Hypoglycemia
Metformin Decreases hepatic glucose production GI upset
Sulfonylureas Increase insulin release Hypoglycemia
Pioglitazone Increases insulin sensitivity Weight gain, edema
DPP-4 inhibitors Increase incretin activity Generally well tolerated
GLP-1 agonists Increase glucose-dependent insulin secretion GI effects
SGLT2 inhibitors Increase urinary glucose excretion Genital infections, volume depletion

5. Thyroid Hormone Drugs

Levothyroxine

Levothyroxine is synthetic T4 and is the standard thyroid hormone replacement for hypothyroidism.

It is converted in peripheral tissues to the more active hormone T3.

Overdose Effects: Symptoms of hyperthyroidism such as palpitations, tremor, sweating, anxiety and weight loss.

Liothyronine

Liothyronine is synthetic T3. It has a faster onset but a shorter duration than levothyroxine and is used in selected situations.

6. Antithyroid Drugs

Thioamides

  • Methimazole
  • Propylthiouracil (PTU)

Thioamides inhibit thyroid peroxidase and reduce thyroid hormone synthesis. PTU also decreases peripheral conversion of T4 to T3.

Important Difference: PTU is preferred over methimazole in the first trimester of pregnancy in many guidelines and is also used in thyroid storm because it additionally reduces peripheral T4-to-T3 conversion.

Other Drugs

  • Radioactive iodine
  • Potassium iodide
  • Beta blockers for symptomatic control

Important Adverse Effects

  • Agranulocytosis
  • Hepatotoxicity
  • Rash

7. Corticosteroids

Corticosteroids have anti-inflammatory and immunosuppressive effects and are used in many endocrine and non-endocrine disorders.

Examples

  • Hydrocortisone
  • Prednisone
  • Prednisolone
  • Methylprednisolone
  • Dexamethasone

Glucocorticoid Effects

  • Decrease inflammation
  • Suppress immune responses
  • Increase blood glucose
  • Increase protein catabolism with prolonged exposure

Adverse Effects of Long-Term Therapy

  • Hyperglycemia
  • Hypertension
  • Osteoporosis
  • Weight gain
  • Increased infection risk
  • Skin thinning
  • Adrenal suppression
  • Cataracts and glaucoma
Important: Long-term systemic corticosteroids should generally not be stopped abruptly because of the risk of adrenal insufficiency. Dose reduction should be individualized.

8. Mineralocorticoids

Fludrocortisone

Fludrocortisone has significant mineralocorticoid activity and is used for selected forms of adrenal insufficiency and orthostatic hypotension.

Mineralocorticoid Effects

  • Increases sodium reabsorption
  • Increases water retention
  • Increases potassium excretion
Adverse Effects: Hypertension, edema and hypokalemia.

9. Sex Hormones

Estrogens

  • Estradiol
  • Ethinyl estradiol

Estrogens are used in hormone replacement therapy and are components of many combined contraceptive preparations.

Progestins

  • Progesterone
  • Levonorgestrel
  • Medroxyprogesterone

Progestins are used in contraception, hormone therapy and selected gynecological conditions.

Androgens

  • Testosterone
  • Testosterone preparations

Androgens are used for selected forms of androgen deficiency.

Hormonal therapy should be selected according to the patient's clinical condition and contraindications.

10. Oral Contraceptives

Combined Oral Contraceptives

Combined oral contraceptives contain an estrogen and a progestin. They primarily prevent ovulation and also produce changes in cervical mucus and the endometrium.

Progestin-Only Contraceptives

  • Progestin-only pills
  • Injectable progestins
  • Hormonal implants
  • Levonorgestrel intrauterine system

Emergency Contraception

  • Levonorgestrel
  • Ulipristal acetate
Important: Combined estrogen-containing contraceptives increase the risk of venous thromboembolism in susceptible individuals.

11. Antiandrogen Drugs

Androgen Receptor Antagonists

  • Flutamide
  • Bicalutamide
  • Enzalutamide

5-Alpha Reductase Inhibitors

  • Finasteride
  • Dutasteride

Finasteride and dutasteride decrease conversion of testosterone to dihydrotestosterone (DHT).

Clinical Uses: Finasteride and dutasteride are used in selected patients with benign prostatic hyperplasia and finasteride is also used for androgenetic alopecia.

12. Pituitary Hormone Drugs

Growth Hormone

Recombinant growth hormone such as somatropin is used for selected growth hormone deficiencies and other approved indications.

Growth Hormone Antagonist

Pegvisomant blocks the growth hormone receptor and is used in selected patients with acromegaly.

Desmopressin

Desmopressin is a vasopressin V2 receptor agonist.

It is used in central diabetes insipidus and selected bleeding disorders such as mild hemophilia A and some cases of von Willebrand disease.

Adverse Effect: Hyponatremia and water retention can occur with excessive desmopressin effect.

13. Drugs Affecting Adrenal Hormones

Drugs That Reduce Cortisol Production

  • Ketoconazole
  • Metyrapone
  • Osilodrostat

Mineralocorticoid Receptor Antagonists

  • Spironolactone
  • Eplerenone

These drugs block aldosterone action and are used in conditions such as primary hyperaldosteronism and selected patients with heart failure.

14. Drugs for Osteoporosis

Bisphosphonates

  • Alendronate
  • Risedronate
  • Zoledronic acid

Bisphosphonates inhibit osteoclast-mediated bone resorption and increase bone mineral density.

Other Drugs

  • Denosumab
  • Teriparatide
  • Abaloparatide
  • Raloxifene
High-Yield: Teriparatide and abaloparatide are anabolic therapies that stimulate bone formation and are used in selected high-risk patients.

15. Calcium and Vitamin D

Vitamin D

  • Cholecalciferol (Vitamin D3)
  • Ergocalciferol (Vitamin D2)
  • Calcitriol

Vitamin D increases intestinal absorption of calcium and phosphate and is important for normal bone mineralization.

Calcium Supplements

  • Calcium carbonate
  • Calcium citrate
Excessive vitamin D or calcium supplementation can contribute to hypercalcemia and other complications.

17. Quick Revision Points

  • Insulin lowers blood glucose and can cause hypoglycemia.
  • Metformin mainly reduces hepatic glucose production.
  • Sulfonylureas increase insulin secretion and can cause hypoglycemia.
  • GLP-1 receptor agonists increase glucose-dependent insulin secretion and promote satiety.
  • SGLT2 inhibitors increase urinary glucose excretion.
  • Levothyroxine is the standard thyroid hormone replacement for hypothyroidism.
  • Methimazole inhibits thyroid hormone synthesis.
  • PTU additionally reduces peripheral T4-to-T3 conversion.
  • Glucocorticoids have anti-inflammatory and immunosuppressive effects.
  • Long-term corticosteroids can cause adrenal suppression and osteoporosis.
  • Fludrocortisone has significant mineralocorticoid activity.
  • Combined oral contraceptives contain estrogen and progestin.
  • Finasteride inhibits 5-alpha reductase.
  • Desmopressin is a V2 receptor agonist.
  • Bisphosphonates reduce osteoclast-mediated bone resorption.
  • Vitamin D increases intestinal calcium and phosphate absorption.

18. Endocrine Pharmacology MCQs

1. Which hormone lowers blood glucose?

A. Glucagon

B. Insulin

C. Cortisol

D. Growth hormone

Answer: B. Insulin
2. Which drug primarily reduces hepatic glucose production?

A. Metformin

B. Insulin

C. Glibenclamide

D. Pioglitazone

Answer: A. Metformin
3. Which drug is the standard thyroid hormone replacement?

A. Methimazole

B. Levothyroxine

C. PTU

D. Potassium iodide

Answer: B. Levothyroxine
4. Which antithyroid drug also decreases peripheral T4 to T3 conversion?

A. Methimazole

B. PTU

C. Levothyroxine

D. Radioactive iodine

Answer: B. PTU
5. Which is a major adverse effect of insulin therapy?

A. Hypercalcemia

B. Hypoglycemia

C. Hyperthyroidism

D. Agranulocytosis

Answer: B. Hypoglycemia
6. Which drug inhibits 5-alpha reductase?

A. Finasteride

B. Testosterone

C. Estrogen

D. Prednisone

Answer: A. Finasteride
7. Desmopressin primarily acts on which receptor?

A. V1

B. V2

C. D2

D. Beta-1

Answer: B. V2
8. Which drug is a bisphosphonate?

A. Alendronate

B. Metformin

C. Methimazole

D. Desmopressin

Answer: A. Alendronate
9. Which drug has significant mineralocorticoid activity?

A. Dexamethasone

B. Fludrocortisone

C. Insulin

D. Levothyroxine

Answer: B. Fludrocortisone
10. Which class increases urinary glucose excretion?

A. SGLT2 inhibitors

B. Sulfonylureas

C. DPP-4 inhibitors

D. Thiazolidinediones

Answer: A. SGLT2 inhibitors
11. Which drug is a GLP-1 receptor agonist?

A. Semaglutide

B. Metformin

C. Glipizide

D. Sitagliptin

Answer: A. Semaglutide
12. Long-term corticosteroid therapy may cause:

A. Osteoporosis

B. Severe hypoglycemia in all patients

C. Hypothyroidism directly

D. Agranulocytosis

Answer: A. Osteoporosis
