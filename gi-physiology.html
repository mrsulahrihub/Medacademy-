🍽️ GI Physiology

Complete high-yield notes about gastrointestinal motility, secretion, digestion and absorption.

1. Introduction to GI Physiology

Gastrointestinal physiology deals with the functions of the digestive system, including movement of food, secretion of digestive juices, digestion, absorption and elimination.

Main functions: Motility → Secretion → Digestion → Absorption → Defecation

2. Gastrointestinal Tract

The gastrointestinal tract is a continuous muscular tube extending from the mouth to the anus.

  • Mouth
  • Pharynx
  • Esophagus
  • Stomach
  • Small intestine
  • Large intestine
  • Rectum
  • Anus

3. Major Functions of the GI System

  • Ingestion of food
  • Propulsion of food
  • Mechanical digestion
  • Chemical digestion
  • Secretion of digestive substances
  • Absorption of nutrients
  • Absorption of water and electrolytes
  • Defecation

4. Salivary Secretion

Saliva is produced mainly by the parotid, submandibular and sublingual glands.

Functions of Saliva

  • Lubricates food
  • Helps swallowing
  • Begins carbohydrate digestion
  • Helps maintain oral hygiene
  • Contributes to taste
Important enzyme: Salivary amylase begins starch digestion.

5. Swallowing (Deglutition)

Swallowing moves food from the mouth to the stomach.

Three Phases

  1. Oral phase
  2. Pharyngeal phase
  3. Esophageal phase
The oral phase is mainly voluntary, while the pharyngeal and esophageal phases are largely reflexive.

6. Gastric Physiology

The stomach stores food, mixes it and begins important aspects of digestion.

Major Gastric Cells

Cell Main Secretion
Parietal cells HCl and intrinsic factor
Chief cells Pepsinogen
G cells Gastrin
Mucous cells Mucus

7. Hydrochloric Acid (HCl)

Gastric HCl is secreted by parietal cells.

Functions

  • Activates pepsinogen to pepsin
  • Provides acidic environment for digestion
  • Helps destroy many microorganisms
  • Facilitates protein digestion
Parietal cell → HCl + Intrinsic Factor

8. Gastrin

Gastrin is produced mainly by G cells of the stomach.

Actions of Gastrin

  • Stimulates gastric acid secretion
  • Promotes gastric mucosal growth
  • Increases gastric motility

9. Pancreatic Secretion

The pancreas releases digestive enzymes and bicarbonate into the small intestine.

Important Components

  • Pancreatic amylase
  • Pancreatic lipase
  • Proteolytic enzymes
  • Bicarbonate
Pancreatic bicarbonate helps neutralize acidic chyme entering the duodenum.

10. Bile

Bile is produced by the liver and stored and concentrated in the gallbladder.

Functions

  • Emulsification of dietary fats
  • Facilitates fat digestion
  • Helps absorption of fat-soluble vitamins
  • Provides a route for excretion of bilirubin

11. Small Intestine

The small intestine is the major site of digestion and nutrient absorption.

Parts

  • Duodenum
  • Jejunum
  • Ileum
Most nutrient absorption occurs in the small intestine.

12. Intestinal Villi and Microvilli

Villi and microvilli greatly increase the surface area available for absorption.

  • Villi contain blood capillaries.
  • Villi also contain lymphatic vessels called lacteals.
  • Microvilli form the brush border.
Lacteals are important for absorption of dietary lipids.

13. Carbohydrate Digestion and Absorption

Carbohydrates are ultimately broken down into monosaccharides.

  • Starch digestion begins with salivary amylase.
  • Pancreatic amylase continues carbohydrate digestion.
  • Brush-border enzymes complete digestion.
  • Glucose and galactose are absorbed mainly through sodium-dependent transport.
  • Fructose is absorbed by facilitated diffusion.

14. Protein Digestion and Absorption

Protein digestion begins in the stomach and continues in the small intestine.

  • Pepsin begins protein digestion in the stomach.
  • Pancreatic proteases digest proteins in the small intestine.
  • Peptidases complete digestion at the intestinal brush border.
  • Amino acids and small peptides are absorbed by intestinal cells.

15. Fat Digestion and Absorption

Fat digestion depends on bile salts and pancreatic digestive enzymes.

  • Bile salts emulsify fats.
  • Pancreatic lipase breaks down triglycerides.
  • Micelles help deliver lipid products to enterocytes.
  • Long-chain fatty acids are reassembled into triglycerides.
  • They are packaged into chylomicrons.
  • Chylomicrons enter lymphatic vessels.
Fat → Micelles → Enterocytes → Chylomicrons → Lymph

16. Important GI Hormones

Hormone Main Action
Gastrin Increases gastric acid secretion and motility
Secretin Stimulates pancreatic bicarbonate secretion
CCK Stimulates pancreatic enzyme secretion and gallbladder contraction
GIP Promotes insulin secretion and reduces gastric activity

17. Gastrointestinal Motility

GI motility moves food through the digestive tract and mixes it with digestive secretions.

Important Types

  • Peristalsis
  • Segmentation
  • Gastric mixing
  • Colonic movements
Peristalsis mainly propels intestinal contents forward.

18. Enteric Nervous System

The enteric nervous system helps control gastrointestinal motility, secretion and blood flow.

Main Plexuses

  • Myenteric plexus
  • Submucosal plexus
Myenteric plexus is particularly important for gastrointestinal motility.

19. Large Intestine

The large intestine is mainly involved in absorption of water and electrolytes and formation of feces.

  • Absorption of water
  • Absorption of electrolytes
  • Storage of fecal material
  • Formation and propulsion of feces
  • Interaction with intestinal bacteria

20. Defecation

Defecation is the process of eliminating fecal material from the rectum through the anus.

  • Rectal distension initiates defecation reflexes.
  • Internal anal sphincter is smooth muscle.
  • External anal sphincter is skeletal muscle.
