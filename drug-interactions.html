Introduction to Drug Interactions

A drug interaction occurs when the effects, concentration, or safety of one drug are altered by another drug, food, disease condition, or other substance.

Key Concept:

Drug interaction = One substance changes the effect or exposure of another substance.

Drug interactions may increase therapeutic effects, reduce treatment effectiveness, or increase the risk of adverse drug reactions.

1. Drug-Drug Interactions

A drug-drug interaction occurs when one medication changes the pharmacological effect or exposure of another medication.

Possible Outcomes

  • Increased therapeutic effect.
  • Decreased therapeutic effect.
  • Increased toxicity.
  • Reduced drug concentration.
  • Increased drug concentration.
Important:

Not every drug interaction is harmful. Some combinations are intentionally used to produce a desired therapeutic effect.

2. Major Types of Drug Interactions

Drug interactions can be broadly divided into pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic interactions.

Type Main Concept Effect
Pharmacokinetic One drug changes the concentration of another drug. Changes absorption, distribution, metabolism or excretion.
Pharmacodynamic Drugs interact at their effects or physiological targets. Effects may be additive, synergistic or antagonistic.

3. Pharmacokinetic Drug Interactions

Pharmacokinetic interactions occur when one drug changes the absorption, distribution, metabolism or excretion of another drug.

Remember ADME:

A = Absorption
D = Distribution
M = Metabolism
E = Excretion

4. Interactions Affecting Absorption

One drug can alter the amount or rate at which another drug is absorbed from the gastrointestinal tract.

Important Mechanisms

  • Changes in gastric pH.
  • Binding or chelation within the gastrointestinal tract.
  • Changes in gastrointestinal motility.
  • Changes in intestinal transporters.
Clinical Example:

Tetracyclines and fluoroquinolones can form complexes with polyvalent cations such as calcium, magnesium or iron, reducing absorption.
Practical Point:

Some medicines should be separated from mineral-containing supplements or antacids according to the specific drug's prescribing information.

5. Interactions Affecting Distribution

Drugs may compete for plasma protein binding sites or alter tissue distribution.

Protein Binding

Many drugs circulate in the blood partly bound to plasma proteins such as albumin.

Drug + Plasma Protein ⇌ Bound Drug

Only the unbound fraction is generally available to leave the plasma and interact with tissues or receptors.

Important:

Changes in protein binding do not always produce a clinically important change in drug effect because the body may compensate through changes in distribution, metabolism or elimination.

6. Drug Metabolism Interactions

Drug metabolism interactions occur when one drug changes the metabolism of another drug, particularly through hepatic enzymes or intestinal enzymes.

The most clinically important mechanisms often involve enzyme induction and enzyme inhibition.

7. Enzyme Induction

Enzyme induction occurs when a drug or other substance increases the amount or activity of drug-metabolizing enzymes.

General Effect:

Enzyme induction → Faster metabolism of susceptible drugs

Faster metabolism may decrease the plasma concentration and effect of some drugs.

Important Enzyme Inducers

  • Rifampin
  • Carbamazepine
  • Phenytoin
  • Phenobarbital
  • St John's wort
Clinical Example:

Rifampin can increase the metabolism of several medicines, including some hormonal contraceptives, potentially reducing their effectiveness.
Exam Point:

Enzyme induction usually develops over several days because increased enzyme production takes time.

8. Enzyme Inhibition

Enzyme inhibition occurs when a drug or substance decreases the activity of a drug-metabolizing enzyme.

General Effect:

Enzyme inhibition → Slower metabolism of susceptible drugs

Slower metabolism may increase plasma concentration and toxicity of some drugs.

Important Enzyme Inhibitors

  • Clarithromycin and erythromycin
  • Azole antifungals such as ketoconazole
  • Some protease inhibitors
  • Grapefruit juice can inhibit intestinal CYP3A4 for certain medicines
Clinical Example:

Macrolide antibiotics such as clarithromycin can inhibit CYP3A4 and may increase concentrations of certain drugs metabolized by this pathway.
Exam Point:

Enzyme inhibition can occur relatively quickly because it does not require the production of new enzymes.

9. Enzyme Induction vs Enzyme Inhibition

Feature Enzyme Induction Enzyme Inhibition
Enzyme activity Increases Decreases
Drug metabolism Usually increases Usually decreases
Effect on substrate drug Often decreases drug concentration Often increases drug concentration
Onset Usually slower Often faster
Major concern Treatment failure Toxicity
Caution:

The clinical effect depends on the specific drug and metabolic pathway. Increased metabolism does not always mean reduced drug effect because some drugs are prodrugs that require metabolism for activation.

10. Prodrugs and Drug Interactions

A prodrug is administered in a form that is converted in the body into an active metabolite.

Important Concept:

For a prodrug:

Increased metabolism may sometimes increase formation of the active drug.

Reduced metabolism may sometimes decrease formation of the active drug.
Example:

Clopidogrel requires metabolic activation, particularly through CYP2C19. Drugs that strongly inhibit this pathway can reduce formation of its active metabolite.

11. Interactions Affecting Excretion

Drug interactions can alter renal or biliary elimination. Renal interactions may involve changes in filtration, secretion or urinary pH.

Important Mechanisms

  • Competition for renal tubular secretion.
  • Changes in urinary pH affecting drug reabsorption.
  • Changes in renal function.
  • Alteration of transport proteins.
Clinical Example:

Probenecid reduces renal tubular secretion of some drugs and has historically been used to prolong the action of certain antibiotics such as penicillin.

12. Pharmacodynamic Drug Interactions

Pharmacodynamic interactions occur when drugs influence each other's effects without necessarily changing their concentrations.

Major Types

  • Additive effects.
  • Synergistic effects.
  • Antagonistic effects.

13. Additive Drug Interactions

An additive interaction occurs when the combined effect of two drugs is approximately equal to the sum of their individual effects.

1 + 1 = 2
Clinical Example:

Two drugs that both lower blood pressure may produce a greater blood-pressure-lowering effect when used together.

14. Synergistic Drug Interactions

Synergism occurs when the combined effect of two drugs is greater than the expected sum of their individual effects.

1 + 1 > 2
Clinical Example:

Trimethoprim and sulfamethoxazole inhibit sequential steps in bacterial folate metabolism and produce a combined antibacterial effect.

15. Pharmacodynamic Antagonism

Antagonism occurs when one drug reduces or opposes the effect of another drug.

Examples

  • Naloxone antagonizes opioid effects at opioid receptors.
  • Flumazenil antagonizes benzodiazepine effects at benzodiazepine receptor sites.
  • Beta-blockers can oppose some effects of beta-adrenergic agonists.
Key Concept:

Pharmacodynamic antagonism can occur at the same receptor or through different physiological pathways.

16. Important Clinical Drug Interactions

Drug Combination Interaction Clinical Concern
Warfarin + NSAIDs Pharmacodynamic effects can increase bleeding risk. Increased bleeding
Opioids + benzodiazepines Additive CNS and respiratory depressant effects. Severe sedation and respiratory depression
Rifampin + hormonal contraceptives Enzyme induction can increase metabolism of contraceptive hormones. Reduced contraceptive effectiveness
Clarithromycin + certain CYP3A4 substrates Enzyme inhibition can increase drug exposure. Potential toxicity
Tetracycline + calcium Chelation reduces gastrointestinal absorption. Reduced antibiotic absorption
Clopidogrel + strong CYP2C19 inhibitor Reduced formation of active metabolite may occur. Reduced antiplatelet effect
Naloxone + opioid Receptor antagonism. Reversal of opioid effects

17. Cytochrome P450 System

The cytochrome P450 (CYP) enzyme system is a major group of enzymes involved in the metabolism of many drugs.

Important CYP Enzymes

  • CYP3A4: Metabolizes many commonly used medicines.
  • CYP2D6: Important for metabolism of several cardiovascular, psychiatric and analgesic drugs.
  • CYP2C19: Important for metabolism of several drugs including clopidogrel activation.
  • CYP2C9: Important in metabolism of drugs such as warfarin.
Exam Point:

CYP enzyme inhibition generally tends to increase the concentration of susceptible substrate drugs, while induction generally tends to decrease their concentration.

18. Drug-Food Interactions

Food and beverages can alter drug absorption, metabolism or pharmacological effects.

Important Example:

Grapefruit juice can inhibit intestinal CYP3A4 and may increase exposure to certain medicines.

The clinical importance depends on the particular medicine, dose and patient factors.

19. Drug-Disease Interactions

A disease condition can change how a drug behaves or can increase the risk of a drug's adverse effects.

Examples

  • Renal impairment can increase exposure to drugs primarily eliminated by the kidneys.
  • Liver disease can alter metabolism of some medicines.
  • Asthma may be worsened by non-selective beta-blockers in susceptible patients.
  • Peptic ulcer disease can increase concern about gastrointestinal effects of NSAIDs.

20. Prevention of Drug Interactions

  • Obtain a complete medication history.
  • Include prescription, over-the-counter and herbal products.
  • Check for important drug-drug interactions.
  • Consider renal and hepatic function.
  • Avoid unnecessary polypharmacy.
  • Monitor drugs with a narrow therapeutic index carefully.
  • Educate patients about important food and medication interactions.
  • Monitor therapeutic response and adverse effects.

21. Drugs with Narrow Therapeutic Index

Some drugs have a small difference between concentrations that produce therapeutic and toxic effects.

Important:

Interactions involving drugs with a narrow therapeutic index can be clinically significant and may require careful monitoring or dose adjustment.
Examples:

Warfarin, digoxin, lithium and some antiepileptic drugs require particular attention to interactions and patient-specific factors.

22. Pharmacokinetic vs Pharmacodynamic Interactions

Feature Pharmacokinetic Pharmacodynamic
Basic concept Changes drug concentration or exposure Changes drug effect
Main processes Absorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion Receptor or physiological effects
Drug level Often changes May remain unchanged
Example Rifampin increasing metabolism of another drug Opioid + benzodiazepine causing additive CNS depression

23. Quick Revision Points

  • Drug interactions can increase, decrease or modify the effect of another drug.
  • Pharmacokinetic interaction = changes drug exposure.
  • Pharmacokinetic interactions involve ADME.
  • Pharmacodynamic interaction = changes drug effects.
  • Enzyme induction generally increases drug metabolism.
  • Enzyme inhibition generally decreases drug metabolism.
  • Enzyme induction usually develops more slowly than enzyme inhibition.
  • Prodrugs are an important exception when considering the effect of altered metabolism.
  • Additive effect ≈ 1 + 1 = 2.
  • Synergistic effect > 1 + 1.
  • Antagonism reduces or opposes another drug's effect.
  • CYP enzymes are important in the metabolism of many drugs.
  • Drugs with narrow therapeutic indices require special attention to interactions.

24. Drug Interactions - Important MCQs

Q1. Pharmacokinetic drug interactions involve:

A. Only receptor binding
B. Absorption, distribution, metabolism and excretion
C. Only drug allergy
D. Only drug toxicity

Answer: B. Absorption, distribution, metabolism and excretion
Q2. Enzyme induction usually causes:

A. Decreased drug metabolism
B. Increased drug metabolism
C. Complete inhibition of absorption
D. Receptor blockade

Answer: B. Increased drug metabolism
Q3. Which is an important enzyme inducer?

A. Rifampin
B. Clarithromycin
C. Ketoconazole
D. Grapefruit juice

Answer: A. Rifampin
Q4. Enzyme inhibition generally causes:

A. Faster metabolism
B. Slower metabolism of susceptible drugs
C. Increased renal filtration only
D. Complete drug absorption

Answer: B. Slower metabolism of susceptible drugs
Q5. Which is an example of pharmacodynamic interaction?

A. Rifampin increasing drug metabolism
B. Tetracycline binding calcium
C. Opioid and benzodiazepine causing additive CNS depression
D. Reduced renal drug excretion

Answer: C. Opioid and benzodiazepine causing additive CNS depression
Q6. The abbreviation ADME refers to:

A. Absorption, Distribution, Metabolism, Excretion
B. Action, Dose, Metabolism, Effect
C. Absorption, Dose, Mechanism, Elimination
D. Action, Distribution, Mechanism, Excretion

Answer: A. Absorption, Distribution, Metabolism, Excretion
Q7. A synergistic drug interaction means:

A. Combined effect is less than expected
B. Combined effect is approximately equal to the sum
C. Combined effect is greater than the expected sum
D. Both drugs become inactive

Answer: C. Combined effect is greater than the expected sum
Q8. Which drug can antagonize opioid effects?

A. Naloxone
B. Rifampin
C. Warfarin
D. Digoxin

Answer: A. Naloxone
Q9. Which combination can increase bleeding risk?

A. Warfarin + NSAID
B. Calcium + tetracycline
C. Naloxone + opioid
D. Rifampin + substrate drug

Answer: A. Warfarin + NSAID
Q10. Which statement about enzyme induction is correct?

A. It usually occurs instantly
B. It generally increases the amount or activity of drug-metabolizing enzymes
C. It always increases drug effect
D. It always decreases toxicity

Answer: B. It generally increases the amount or activity of drug-metabolizing enzymes
