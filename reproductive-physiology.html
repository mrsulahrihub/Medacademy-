♀️♂️ Reproductive Physiology

Complete exam-focused notes covering male and female reproductive physiology, hormonal regulation, menstrual cycle, pregnancy and lactation.

1. Introduction

Reproductive physiology explains the physiological processes involved in reproduction, including formation of gametes, reproductive hormones, menstrual cycle, fertilization, pregnancy and lactation.

High-Yield Concept: Reproductive function is mainly regulated through the hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal (HPG) axis.

2. Male Reproductive Physiology

The male reproductive system produces spermatozoa and testosterone and provides the physiological mechanisms required for reproduction.

Major Functions

  • Production of sperm
  • Production of testosterone
  • Transport of sperm
  • Support of sexual function

3. Spermatogenesis

Spermatogenesis is the process by which spermatogonia develop into mature spermatozoa within the seminiferous tubules of the testes.

Main Stages

  • Spermatogonia
  • Primary spermatocytes
  • Secondary spermatocytes
  • Spermatids
  • Spermatozoa
Spermatogenesis occurs in the seminiferous tubules of the testes.

4. Sertoli and Leydig Cells

Cell Main Function
Sertoli cells Support developing sperm cells and help regulate spermatogenesis.
Leydig cells Produce testosterone.

5. Testosterone

Testosterone is the major androgen produced primarily by Leydig cells of the testes.

Major Functions

  • Development of male reproductive organs
  • Development of male secondary sexual characteristics
  • Support of spermatogenesis
  • Maintenance of libido
  • Effects on muscle and bone

6. Hypothalamic-Pituitary-Gonadal Axis

The HPG axis controls reproductive hormone secretion through a coordinated hormonal pathway.

Hypothalamus → GnRH

Anterior Pituitary → LH + FSH

Gonads → Testosterone / Estrogen / Progesterone

Gonadal hormones provide negative feedback to the hypothalamus and pituitary under most physiological conditions.

7. Female Reproductive Physiology

Female reproductive physiology involves ovarian follicle development, ovulation, menstrual cycling, hormone production and preparation of the reproductive tract for pregnancy.

Major Processes

  • Oogenesis
  • Follicular development
  • Ovulation
  • Corpus luteum formation
  • Menstrual cycle
  • Preparation for pregnancy

8. Ovarian Cycle

The ovarian cycle can be divided into three major phases.

Phase Main Events Main Hormonal Association
Follicular Phase Follicle development FSH and estrogen
Ovulation Release of secondary oocyte LH surge
Luteal Phase Corpus luteum activity Progesterone

9. Follicular Phase

During the follicular phase, ovarian follicles develop under the influence of gonadotropins.

  • FSH supports follicular development.
  • Growing follicles produce estrogen.
  • Estrogen promotes proliferation of the endometrium.

10. Ovulation and LH Surge

Ovulation is the release of a secondary oocyte from the dominant ovarian follicle.

Important: A sustained high estrogen level before ovulation contributes to positive feedback and triggers the characteristic LH surge.

The LH surge is the major hormonal event associated with ovulation.

11. Luteal Phase

After ovulation, the ruptured follicle develops into the corpus luteum.

  • Corpus luteum produces progesterone.
  • Progesterone supports the secretory endometrium.
  • Estrogen is also produced.
  • Without pregnancy, the corpus luteum eventually regresses.

12. Menstrual Cycle

The menstrual cycle involves coordinated changes in the ovaries and endometrium.

Phase Key Feature
Menstrual Phase Shedding of the functional endometrium.
Proliferative Phase Endometrial growth under estrogen influence.
Secretory Phase Endometrium becomes secretory under progesterone influence.

13. Estrogen and Progesterone

Estrogen

  • Promotes development of female secondary sexual characteristics.
  • Stimulates endometrial proliferation.
  • Contributes to reproductive tract function.

Progesterone

  • Supports the secretory endometrium.
  • Helps prepare the uterus for implantation.
  • Supports pregnancy after implantation.

14. Oogenesis

Oogenesis is the process of formation and maturation of female gametes.

  • Oogonia develop during fetal life.
  • Primary oocytes remain arrested for prolonged periods.
  • During reproductive life, selected follicles resume development.
  • Ovulation releases a secondary oocyte.

15. Fertilization

Fertilization is the fusion of male and female gametes resulting in formation of a zygote.

  • Sperm reaches the female reproductive tract.
  • Sperm undergoes capacitation.
  • Sperm penetrates the oocyte coverings.
  • Fusion of gamete genetic material forms the zygote.

16. Pregnancy Physiology

Pregnancy produces major physiological and hormonal changes that support fetal development and prepare the mother for childbirth and lactation.

Important Hormonal Changes

  • hCG supports the early corpus luteum.
  • Placenta becomes an important endocrine organ.
  • Estrogen and progesterone levels increase.
  • Maternal physiological systems adapt to pregnancy.

17. hCG

Human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG) is produced by placental trophoblastic tissue during early pregnancy.

High-Yield Point: hCG helps maintain the corpus luteum during early pregnancy, allowing continued progesterone production.

18. Placental Hormones

The placenta acts as an endocrine organ and produces several hormones important for maintenance of pregnancy.

  • hCG
  • Estrogens
  • Progesterone
  • Human placental lactogen (hPL)

19. Lactation

Lactation is the production and ejection of milk after childbirth.

Prolactin

Prolactin promotes milk production by the mammary glands.

Oxytocin

Oxytocin causes contraction of myoepithelial cells and contributes to milk ejection.

Remember: Prolactin → Milk Production
Oxytocin → Milk Ejection

20. Menopause

Menopause is the permanent cessation of menstruation resulting from loss of ovarian follicular activity.

  • Ovarian estrogen production decreases.
  • Progesterone production decreases.
  • FSH and LH levels generally increase because of reduced negative feedback.
  • Ovulation and menstruation cease.

⭐ Important Exam Points

  • GnRH is released from the hypothalamus.
  • LH and FSH are secreted by the anterior pituitary.
  • Leydig cells produce testosterone.
  • Sertoli cells support spermatogenesis.
  • FSH is important for follicular development.
  • The LH surge is associated with ovulation.
  • Corpus luteum produces progesterone.
  • Estrogen promotes endometrial proliferation.
  • Progesterone supports the secretory endometrium.
  • hCG supports the corpus luteum during early pregnancy.
  • Prolactin promotes milk production.
  • Oxytocin promotes milk ejection.
