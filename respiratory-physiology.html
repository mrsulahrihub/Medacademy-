🫁 Respiratory Physiology

Ventilation, gas exchange, oxygen transport and regulation of breathing

1. Introduction

Respiratory physiology is the study of how the respiratory system performs ventilation, gas exchange and transport of respiratory gases.

The main purpose of respiration is to provide oxygen to the body and remove carbon dioxide produced by metabolism.

Main functions:
  • Ventilation
  • Gas exchange
  • Oxygen transport
  • Carbon dioxide removal
  • Regulation of acid-base balance

2. Pulmonary Ventilation

Pulmonary ventilation is the movement of air between the atmosphere and the alveoli.

Inspiration

  • Diaphragm contracts and moves downward.
  • Thoracic volume increases.
  • Intrapulmonary pressure decreases.
  • Air moves into the lungs.

Expiration

  • Diaphragm relaxes during quiet breathing.
  • Thoracic volume decreases.
  • Intrapulmonary pressure increases.
  • Air moves out of the lungs.

3. Boyle's Law

At constant temperature, pressure and volume of a gas are inversely related.

P ∝ 1 / V
↑ Volume → ↓ Pressure → Air enters

↓ Volume → ↑ Pressure → Air leaves

4. Lung Volumes

Volume Meaning
Tidal Volume (TV) Air inspired or expired during a normal quiet breath
Inspiratory Reserve Volume (IRV) Additional air that can be inspired after a normal inspiration
Expiratory Reserve Volume (ERV) Additional air that can be expired after a normal expiration
Residual Volume (RV) Air remaining in the lungs after maximal expiration

5. Lung Capacities

Capacity Formula
Inspiratory Capacity TV + IRV
Functional Residual Capacity ERV + RV
Vital Capacity IRV + TV + ERV
Total Lung Capacity IRV + TV + ERV + RV

6. Alveoli and Gas Exchange

Alveoli are tiny air sacs where oxygen and carbon dioxide are exchanged between alveolar air and pulmonary capillary blood.

Gas Exchange

  • Oxygen moves from alveoli into blood.
  • Carbon dioxide moves from blood into alveoli.

Gas exchange occurs mainly by diffusion down partial pressure gradients.

7. Diffusion of Respiratory Gases

Diffusion is the movement of molecules from an area of higher partial pressure toward an area of lower partial pressure.

O₂: Alveoli → Pulmonary blood

CO₂: Pulmonary blood → Alveoli

8. Pulmonary Surfactant

Surfactant is produced mainly by type II alveolar cells.

It reduces surface tension inside the alveoli and helps prevent alveolar collapse.

High-yield: Surfactant ↓ surface tension → helps keep alveoli open.

9. Oxygen Transport

Oxygen is transported in blood mainly bound to hemoglobin.

Two Forms

  • Most oxygen is transported bound to hemoglobin.
  • A small amount is dissolved in plasma.
Hemoglobin + O₂ → Oxyhemoglobin

10. Carbon Dioxide Transport

Carbon dioxide is transported from tissues to the lungs in several forms.

  • Dissolved in plasma
  • Bound to proteins including hemoglobin
  • Mostly as bicarbonate ions (HCO₃⁻)
Most CO₂ is transported as bicarbonate.

11. Respiratory Membrane

The respiratory membrane is the thin barrier across which gases diffuse between alveolar air and pulmonary capillary blood.

Important Features

  • Very thin diffusion barrier
  • Large surface area
  • Close relationship between alveoli and capillaries

12. Ventilation-Perfusion Relationship

Ventilation refers to airflow reaching the alveoli, while perfusion refers to blood flow through pulmonary capillaries.

Effective gas exchange requires appropriate matching of ventilation and perfusion.

13. Regulation of Breathing

Breathing is regulated by respiratory centers in the brainstem and influenced by chemical signals in the blood.

Main Chemoreceptor Stimulus

Changes in carbon dioxide and hydrogen ion concentration are important in regulation of ventilation.

Brainstem

  • Medulla helps generate the basic respiratory rhythm.
  • Pons helps modify and coordinate breathing.

14. Respiratory Muscles

Main Muscle of Inspiration

Diaphragm is the principal muscle of quiet inspiration.

Other Inspiratory Muscles

  • External intercostal muscles
  • Accessory muscles during increased respiratory effort

Forced Expiration

  • Internal intercostal muscles
  • Abdominal muscles

15. Respiratory Rate

Respiratory rate is the number of breaths taken per minute.

In a healthy resting adult, it is commonly around 12–20 breaths per minute.

Factors Affecting Respiratory Rate

  • Exercise
  • Body temperature
  • Blood gases
  • Emotions
  • Acid-base status

16. Respiratory Role in Acid-Base Balance

The respiratory system helps regulate blood pH by controlling the amount of carbon dioxide removed from the body.

↑ Ventilation → ↓ CO₂ → tendency toward ↑ pH

↓ Ventilation → ↑ CO₂ → tendency toward ↓ pH

⭐ Important Exam Points

  • Main muscle of quiet inspiration = diaphragm.
  • Oxygen moves from alveoli to blood by diffusion.
  • Carbon dioxide moves from blood to alveoli.
  • Surfactant is produced mainly by type II alveolar cells.
  • Surfactant reduces surface tension.
  • Most oxygen is transported bound to hemoglobin.
  • Most carbon dioxide is transported as bicarbonate.
  • Medulla is important for generating the basic respiratory rhythm.
  • Pons helps modify and coordinate breathing.
  • Increased ventilation generally lowers arterial CO₂.
  • Residual volume is the air remaining after maximal expiration.
