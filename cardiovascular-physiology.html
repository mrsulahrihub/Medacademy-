❤️ Cardiovascular Physiology

Cardiac cycle, cardiac output, blood pressure and ECG

1. Introduction

Cardiovascular physiology is the study of how the heart and blood vessels work together to maintain adequate blood flow to body tissues.

The cardiovascular system delivers oxygen and nutrients, removes metabolic waste and helps maintain homeostasis.

Main components:
  • Heart
  • Blood vessels
  • Blood

2. Functions of the Heart

  • Maintains blood circulation.
  • Delivers oxygen and nutrients to tissues.
  • Removes carbon dioxide and metabolic waste.
  • Maintains adequate tissue perfusion.
  • Helps maintain blood pressure.

3. Cardiac Cycle

The cardiac cycle is the sequence of events that occurs during one complete heartbeat.

Main Phases

  1. Atrial systole — atria contract and help fill the ventricles.
  2. Ventricular systole — ventricles contract and eject blood.
  3. Ventricular diastole — ventricles relax and fill with blood.
Systole = contraction
Diastole = relaxation

4. Heart Rate

Heart rate is the number of heartbeats occurring per minute.

In a resting healthy adult, heart rate is commonly around 60–100 beats per minute.

Factors Affecting Heart Rate

  • Autonomic nervous system
  • Exercise
  • Body temperature
  • Hormones
  • Emotions

5. Stroke Volume

Stroke volume is the amount of blood ejected by one ventricle during a single heartbeat.

Stroke Volume = End-Diastolic Volume − End-Systolic Volume

Important Factors

  • Preload
  • Afterload
  • Contractility

6. Cardiac Output

Cardiac output is the volume of blood pumped by one ventricle per minute.

Cardiac Output = Heart Rate × Stroke Volume

For example, if heart rate is 70 beats/minute and stroke volume is 70 mL/beat:

CO = 70 × 70 = 4900 mL/min

Therefore, cardiac output is approximately 4.9 L/min in this example.

7. Preload

Preload refers to the degree of ventricular filling or myocardial fiber stretch at the end of diastole.

Increased venous return generally increases preload.

8. Afterload

Afterload is the resistance against which the ventricles must eject blood.

Systemic vascular resistance and arterial pressure are important determinants of left ventricular afterload.

9. Blood Pressure

Blood pressure is the force exerted by circulating blood against the walls of blood vessels.

Two Main Values

Value Meaning
Systolic Blood Pressure Pressure during ventricular systole
Diastolic Blood Pressure Pressure during ventricular diastole

10. Pulse Pressure

Pulse pressure is the difference between systolic and diastolic blood pressure.

Pulse Pressure = Systolic BP − Diastolic BP

11. Mean Arterial Pressure

Mean arterial pressure (MAP) represents the average pressure driving blood through the systemic circulation during one cardiac cycle.

MAP ≈ Diastolic BP + 1/3(Pulse Pressure)

This approximation is most useful when the heart rate is relatively normal.

12. ECG

Electrocardiogram (ECG) records the electrical activity of the heart from the body surface.

Main ECG Components

Component Represents
P Wave Atrial depolarization
QRS Complex Ventricular depolarization
T Wave Ventricular repolarization
High-yield: P = Atrial depolarization
QRS = Ventricular depolarization
T = Ventricular repolarization

13. Heart Sounds

Heart sounds are mainly produced by vibrations associated with closure of the heart valves.

Sound Main Event
S1 Closure of AV valves
S2 Closure of semilunar valves
S1 = Mitral + Tricuspid closure

S2 = Aortic + Pulmonary closure

14. Autonomic Regulation of the Heart

Sympathetic Stimulation

  • Increases heart rate.
  • Increases cardiac contractility.
  • Increases cardiac output.

Parasympathetic Stimulation

  • Decreases heart rate.
  • Mainly acts through the vagus nerve.

15. Blood Vessels

Vessel Main Function
Arteries Carry blood away from the heart
Arterioles Major resistance vessels
Capillaries Exchange of gases, nutrients and waste
Veins Return blood toward the heart

⭐ Important Exam Points

  • Systole means contraction.
  • Diastole means relaxation.
  • Cardiac output = Heart Rate × Stroke Volume.
  • Stroke volume = EDV − ESV.
  • Pulse pressure = Systolic BP − Diastolic BP.
  • P wave = atrial depolarization.
  • QRS complex = ventricular depolarization.
  • T wave = ventricular repolarization.
  • S1 is mainly due to AV valve closure.
  • S2 is mainly due to semilunar valve closure.
  • Sympathetic stimulation generally increases heart rate and contractility.
  • Parasympathetic stimulation generally decreases heart rate.

📝 Quick Revision

Cardiac Cycle → One complete heartbeat

Systole → Contraction

Diastole → Relaxation

Stroke Volume → Blood ejected per beat

Cardiac Output → HR × SV

Pulse Pressure → SBP − DBP

P Wave → Atrial depolarization

QRS → Ventricular depolarization

T Wave → Ventricular repolarization

S1 → AV valve closure

S2 → Semilunar valve closure
