🧪 Endocrine Physiology

High-yield notes about hormones, endocrine glands, feedback mechanisms and hormonal regulation.

1. Introduction to Endocrine Physiology

Endocrine physiology deals with hormones and their effects on target cells and organs. Hormones help regulate metabolism, growth, reproduction, fluid balance and many other body functions.

Hormone: A chemical messenger released by endocrine cells that acts on specific target cells.

2. Endocrine vs Exocrine Glands

Endocrine Glands Exocrine Glands
Release hormones into blood Release secretions through ducts
No ducts Have ducts
Examples: thyroid, pituitary, adrenal Examples: salivary and sweat glands

  • Norepinephrine

4. Hormonal Feedback Mechanisms

Feedback mechanisms regulate hormone secretion and maintain homeostasis.

Negative Feedback

The final effect of a hormone reduces further stimulation of that hormone's secretion.

Most endocrine systems are regulated primarily by negative feedback.

Positive Feedback

The response increases the original stimulus.

Example: Oxytocin during labor.

5. Pituitary Gland

The pituitary gland is closely connected with the hypothalamus and regulates several endocrine organs.

Anterior Pituitary Hormones

Hormone Main Function
GH Growth and metabolic effects
TSH Stimulates thyroid gland
ACTH Stimulates adrenal cortex
FSH Reproductive functions and gamete development
LH Ovulation and gonadal hormone production
Prolactin Promotes milk production

6. Posterior Pituitary

The posterior pituitary stores and releases hormones produced in the hypothalamus.

Hormone Main Function
ADH Increases renal water reabsorption
Oxytocin Uterine contraction and milk ejection

7. Thyroid Hormones

The thyroid gland produces thyroid hormones that have major effects on metabolism, growth and development.

Important Hormones

  • Thyroxine (T4)
  • Triiodothyronine (T3)
  • Calcitonin

Effects of T3 and T4

  • Increase basal metabolic activity
  • Support normal growth and development
  • Increase responsiveness to catecholamines
T3 is more biologically active than T4.

8. Parathyroid Hormone (PTH)

Parathyroid hormone is secreted by the parathyroid glands and is important for calcium homeostasis.

Major Effects

  • Increases blood calcium concentration
  • Promotes renal calcium reabsorption
  • Reduces renal phosphate reabsorption
  • Promotes activation of vitamin D in the kidney
PTH → increases serum Ca²⁺

9. Adrenal Gland

The adrenal gland consists of the adrenal cortex and adrenal medulla.

Part Major Hormones
Adrenal cortex Aldosterone, cortisol and adrenal androgens
Adrenal medulla Epinephrine and norepinephrine

10. Cortisol

Cortisol is a glucocorticoid produced by the adrenal cortex.

Major Effects

  • Helps maintain blood glucose availability
  • Influences protein and lipid metabolism
  • Has important effects on immune and inflammatory responses
  • Helps the body respond to stress

11. Aldosterone

Aldosterone is a mineralocorticoid produced by the adrenal cortex.

  • Increases sodium reabsorption in the kidney
  • Promotes potassium secretion
  • Helps maintain extracellular fluid volume
  • Contributes to blood pressure regulation
Aldosterone → Na⁺ retention + K⁺ secretion

12. Pancreatic Hormones

Hormone Produced By Main Function
Insulin Beta cells Decreases blood glucose
Glucagon Alpha cells Increases blood glucose
Somatostatin Delta cells Inhibits several endocrine and GI secretions

13. Insulin

Insulin is an anabolic hormone that helps cells take up and store nutrients.

Major Effects

  • Decreases blood glucose
  • Promotes glucose uptake in skeletal muscle and adipose tissue
  • Promotes glycogen synthesis
  • Promotes fat synthesis
  • Promotes protein synthesis
Insulin → lowers blood glucose

14. Glucagon

Glucagon is secreted by pancreatic alpha cells and generally acts opposite to insulin.

  • Increases blood glucose
  • Stimulates glycogen breakdown
  • Stimulates gluconeogenesis
  • Promotes mobilization of energy stores
Glucagon → raises blood glucose

15. Calcium Homeostasis

Calcium is essential for muscle contraction, nerve transmission, blood clotting and bone health.

Important Regulators

  • Parathyroid hormone (PTH)
  • Vitamin D
  • Calcitonin
PTH is a major hormone responsible for maintaining adequate extracellular calcium concentration.

16. Reproductive Hormones

Hormone Important Role
FSH Gamete development
LH Gonadal hormone production and ovulation
Estrogen Female reproductive development and reproductive cycle
Progesterone Supports endometrium and pregnancy
Testosterone Male reproductive development and functions

17. Pineal Gland

The pineal gland secretes melatonin.

Melatonin is involved in regulation of the sleep-wake and circadian rhythms.

18. Hypothalamus

The hypothalamus connects the nervous and endocrine systems and plays a central role in homeostasis.

Functions

  • Regulates pituitary hormone secretion
  • Controls body temperature
  • Regulates thirst
  • Participates in appetite regulation
  • Controls aspects of autonomic function
  • Produces ADH and oxytocin

⭐ Important Exam Points

  • Pituitary gland is closely regulated by the hypothalamus.
  • Anterior pituitary secretes GH, TSH, ACTH, FSH, LH and prolactin.
  • Posterior pituitary releases ADH and oxytocin.
  • ADH increases renal water reabsorption.
  • Thyroid produces T3 and T4.
  • T3 is more biologically active than T4.
  • PTH increases serum calcium.
  • Adrenal cortex produces aldosterone and cortisol.
  • Adrenal medulla produces epinephrine and norepinephrine.
  • Insulin decreases blood glucose.
  • Glucagon increases blood glucose.
  • Aldosterone promotes sodium retention and potassium secretion.
  • Negative feedback is the major regulatory mechanism in endocrine physiology.
