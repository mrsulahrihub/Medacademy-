🫘 Renal Physiology

Complete high-yield notes about kidney function, nephron, urine formation and fluid balance.

1. Introduction to Renal Physiology

Renal physiology is the study of how the kidneys maintain the internal environment of the body. The kidneys regulate water, electrolytes, acid-base balance and remove metabolic waste products.

High-Yield: The functional unit of the kidney is the nephron.

2. Functions of the Kidneys

  • Formation of urine
  • Excretion of metabolic waste products
  • Regulation of water balance
  • Regulation of electrolytes
  • Maintenance of acid-base balance
  • Regulation of blood volume and blood pressure
  • Production of erythropoietin
  • Activation of vitamin D
  • Regulation of calcium and phosphate balance

3. Nephron Structure

A nephron is the basic structural and functional unit of the kidney.

Main Parts of Nephron

  • Renal corpuscle
  • Glomerulus
  • Bowman's capsule
  • Proximal convoluted tubule (PCT)
  • Loop of Henle
  • Distal convoluted tubule (DCT)
  • Collecting duct
Renal corpuscle = Glomerulus + Bowman's capsule

4. Glomerular Filtration

Glomerular filtration is the movement of water and small dissolved substances from the glomerular capillaries into Bowman's capsule.

Filtration Barrier

  • Fenestrated endothelium
  • Glomerular basement membrane
  • Podocyte slit diaphragms

Large proteins and blood cells are normally retained in the blood.

5. Glomerular Filtration Rate (GFR)

GFR is the amount of filtrate formed by both kidneys per unit time.

Normal GFR ≈ 125 mL/min

Factors Affecting GFR

  • Glomerular capillary pressure
  • Plasma colloid osmotic pressure
  • Bowman's capsule pressure
  • Filtration coefficient

6. Tubular Reabsorption

Tubular reabsorption is the movement of substances from tubular fluid back into the blood.

Important Reabsorbed Substances

  • Water
  • Glucose
  • Amino acids
  • Sodium
  • Chloride
  • Bicarbonate
Most filtered sodium and water are reabsorbed in the proximal convoluted tubule.

7. Tubular Secretion

Tubular secretion is the movement of substances from the blood into the renal tubule.

Examples

  • Hydrogen ions (H⁺)
  • Potassium ions (K⁺)
  • Ammonium
  • Creatinine
  • Some drugs and organic acids

8. Loop of Henle

The loop of Henle plays an important role in concentrating urine.

Part Main Function
Descending limb Highly permeable to water
Ascending limb Reabsorbs Na⁺, K⁺ and Cl⁻ and is relatively impermeable to water

9. Countercurrent Mechanism

The countercurrent mechanism helps the kidney establish and maintain a medullary osmotic gradient.

Important Structures

  • Loop of Henle
  • Vasa recta
  • Collecting ducts
This mechanism allows the kidneys to produce concentrated urine when water conservation is required.

10. ADH and Water Reabsorption

Antidiuretic hormone (ADH), also called vasopressin, increases water reabsorption in the kidney.

  • ADH acts mainly on the late distal tubule and collecting ducts.
  • It increases water permeability.
  • It promotes insertion of aquaporin-2 channels.
  • It decreases water loss in urine.
High ADH → more water reabsorbed → concentrated urine

11. Renin-Angiotensin-Aldosterone System (RAAS)

RAAS is an important system involved in the regulation of blood pressure, sodium balance and extracellular fluid volume.

Basic Sequence

  • Renin is released by the kidney.
  • Renin converts angiotensinogen to angiotensin I.
  • ACE converts angiotensin I to angiotensin II.
  • Angiotensin II promotes vasoconstriction.
  • Angiotensin II stimulates aldosterone secretion.
  • Aldosterone increases sodium reabsorption.
RAAS activation generally increases blood pressure and extracellular fluid volume.

12. Regulation of Water and Electrolytes

The kidneys maintain the concentration of electrolytes within a relatively narrow range.

  • Sodium (Na⁺)
  • Potassium (K⁺)
  • Calcium (Ca²⁺)
  • Phosphate
  • Chloride (Cl⁻)
  • Bicarbonate (HCO₃⁻)

13. Renal Role in Acid-Base Balance

The kidneys help maintain blood pH by regulating hydrogen ions and bicarbonate.

  • Secretion of H⁺
  • Reabsorption of filtered bicarbonate
  • Generation of new bicarbonate
  • Excretion of acid as ammonium and other titratable acids
The kidneys provide an important long-term mechanism for maintaining acid-base balance.

14. Formation of Urine

Urine formation involves three major processes:

Process Description
Glomerular filtration Filtration of plasma into Bowman's capsule
Tubular reabsorption Movement of useful substances back into blood
Tubular secretion Movement of selected substances from blood into tubule

⭐ Important Exam Points

  • Functional unit of kidney = Nephron
  • Renal corpuscle = Glomerulus + Bowman's capsule
  • Normal GFR ≈ 125 mL/min
  • PCT is a major site of tubular reabsorption
  • Descending limb is highly permeable to water
  • Ascending limb is relatively impermeable to water
  • Loop of Henle is important for urine concentration
  • ADH increases water reabsorption
  • Aldosterone increases sodium reabsorption
  • Kidneys play an important role in acid-base regulation
  • Kidneys produce erythropoietin
  • Kidneys activate vitamin D
