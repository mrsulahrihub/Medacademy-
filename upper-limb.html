💪 Upper Limb Anatomy

Complete high-yield notes for medical students.

📖 Introduction

The upper limb is attached to the trunk through the shoulder region. It is mainly responsible for movement, positioning of the hand and performing fine motor activities.

The major regions of the upper limb are the shoulder, arm, forearm and hand.

🦴 1. Bones of Upper Limb

The bones of the upper limb provide support and allow movement.

  • Clavicle
  • Scapula
  • Humerus
  • Radius
  • Ulna
  • Carpal bones
  • Metacarpal bones
  • Phalanges

Important Bones

Bone Important Point
Clavicle Connects the upper limb to the axial skeleton.
Scapula Flat triangular bone of the shoulder region.
Humerus Main bone of the arm.
Radius Located on the lateral side of the forearm in anatomical position.
Ulna Located on the medial side of the forearm in anatomical position.

💪 2. Muscles of Upper Limb

The muscles of the upper limb produce movements of the shoulder, arm, forearm and hand.

Major Muscles

  • Deltoid
  • Biceps brachii
  • Triceps brachii
  • Brachialis
  • Coracobrachialis
  • Flexor muscles of forearm
  • Extensor muscles of forearm

Important Functions

Muscle Main Action
Deltoid Mainly abducts the arm.
Biceps brachii Flexes the elbow and assists supination.
Triceps brachii Extends the elbow.
Brachialis Important flexor of the elbow.

🧠 3. Nerves of Upper Limb

The major nerves supplying the upper limb arise from the brachial plexus.

  • Musculocutaneous nerve
  • Axillary nerve
  • Radial nerve
  • Median nerve
  • Ulnar nerve

Important Nerve Functions

Nerve Important Association
Musculocutaneous Supplies major muscles of the anterior arm.
Axillary Associated with the deltoid region.
Radial Major nerve of the posterior arm and forearm.
Median Important nerve of the anterior forearm and hand.
Ulnar Important nerve supplying intrinsic muscles of the hand.

🩸 4. Arteries

The main arterial supply of the upper limb is derived from the subclavian artery.

  • Subclavian artery
  • Axillary artery
  • Brachial artery
  • Radial artery
  • Ulnar artery

Basic Continuity

Subclavian artery → Axillary artery → Brachial artery → Radial and Ulnar arteries

🩸 5. Veins

The upper limb contains superficial and deep venous systems.

  • Cephalic vein
  • Basilic vein
  • Median cubital vein

Clinical Importance

The median cubital vein is commonly used for venipuncture because of its accessibility in the cubital region.

🦴 6. Major Joints

Joint Important Feature
Shoulder joint Connects the humerus with the scapula.
Elbow joint Allows major flexion and extension movements.
Wrist joint Connects the forearm with the hand.

⭐ Important Exam Points

  • Know the bones of the upper limb in sequence.
  • Remember the five major terminal nerves of the brachial plexus.
  • Know the major actions of biceps, triceps and deltoid.
  • Remember the arterial sequence from subclavian to radial and ulnar arteries.
  • Know the superficial veins used clinically.

⚡ Quick Revision

  • Upper limb bones → Clavicle, Scapula, Humerus, Radius, Ulna, Carpals, Metacarpals, Phalanges
  • Major nerves → Musculocutaneous, Axillary, Radial, Median, Ulnar
  • Main artery sequence → Subclavian → Axillary → Brachial → Radial/Ulnar
  • Major superficial veins → Cephalic, Basilic, Median cubital
  • Major joints → Shoulder, Elbow, Wrist
