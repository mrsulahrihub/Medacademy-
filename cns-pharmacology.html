CNS Pharmacology

MedAcademy - Medical Students

1. Introduction to CNS Pharmacology

CNS pharmacology deals with drugs that act on the brain and spinal cord. These drugs are used to treat neurological and psychiatric disorders.

Major CNS Drug Groups:
  • General anesthetics
  • Sedative-hypnotic drugs
  • Antiepileptic drugs
  • Antiparkinsonian drugs
  • Antipsychotic drugs
  • Antidepressant drugs
  • Opioid analgesics
  • CNS stimulants
  • Drugs for Alzheimer's disease
  • Drugs for migraine

2. Important CNS Neurotransmitters

Neurotransmitter Important Functions
GABA Major inhibitory neurotransmitter
Glutamate Major excitatory neurotransmitter
Dopamine Movement, reward, cognition
Serotonin (5-HT) Mood, sleep, appetite
Norepinephrine Alertness, attention, mood
Acetylcholine Memory, learning, neuromuscular function
High-Yield Point: GABA generally inhibits neuronal activity, while glutamate generally increases neuronal excitation.

3. General Anesthetic Drugs

General anesthetics produce reversible loss of consciousness, sensation and responsiveness during surgery.

Inhalational Anesthetics

  • Sevoflurane
  • Isoflurane
  • Desflurane
  • Nitrous oxide

Intravenous Anesthetics

  • Propofol
  • Etomidate
  • Ketamine
  • Thiopental
Important: Propofol is commonly used for induction of anesthesia and causes rapid onset and recovery.

Ketamine

Ketamine produces dissociative anesthesia and is an NMDA receptor antagonist. It may increase blood pressure and heart rate and can cause emergence reactions.

4. Sedative and Hypnotic Drugs

These drugs reduce CNS activity and are used for anxiety, insomnia, seizures and other conditions.

Benzodiazepines

  • Diazepam
  • Lorazepam
  • Midazolam
  • Alprazolam
  • Clonazepam

Benzodiazepines enhance the effect of GABA at the GABA-A receptor and increase the frequency of chloride channel opening.

Barbiturates

  • Phenobarbital
  • Pentobarbital

Barbiturates enhance GABA-A receptor activity and can increase the duration of chloride channel opening.

Important Difference: Benzodiazepines → increase frequency of Cl⁻ channel opening.
Barbiturates → increase duration of Cl⁻ channel opening.

Antidote

Flumazenil is a benzodiazepine receptor antagonist and can reverse benzodiazepine effects in selected situations.

5. Antiepileptic Drugs

Antiepileptic drugs reduce abnormal neuronal activity and prevent or control seizures.

Drug Important Use / Feature
Valproate Broad-spectrum antiepileptic; important pregnancy-related safety concerns
Carbamazepine Focal seizures; trigeminal neuralgia
Phenytoin Focal and generalized tonic-clonic seizures
Lamotrigine Broad-spectrum; also used in bipolar disorder
Levetiracetam Broad clinical use; relatively few drug interactions
Ethosuximide Absence seizures
Phenobarbital Seizures; barbiturate
Benzodiazepines Acute seizure emergencies

Phenytoin

Phenytoin blocks voltage-gated sodium channels and prolongs their inactive state.

Adverse Effects: Nystagmus, ataxia, gingival hyperplasia, hirsutism and rash.

Valproate

Valproate has broad-spectrum antiepileptic activity. It is associated with hepatotoxicity, pancreatitis, weight gain and neural tube defects in pregnancy.

Ethosuximide

Ethosuximide is especially useful for absence seizures and blocks T-type calcium channels in thalamic neurons.

Seizure Emergency: Benzodiazepines such as lorazepam or diazepam are commonly used initially in status epilepticus, followed by appropriate longer-acting therapy when needed.

6. Drugs for Parkinson's Disease

Parkinson's disease is associated with degeneration of dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra, causing reduced dopamine activity in the basal ganglia.

Levodopa + Carbidopa

Levodopa crosses the blood-brain barrier and is converted into dopamine. Carbidopa inhibits peripheral conversion of levodopa to dopamine.

Levodopa: Increases dopamine in the CNS.
Carbidopa: Reduces peripheral conversion of levodopa.

Dopamine Agonists

  • Pramipexole
  • Ropinirole

MAO-B Inhibitors

  • Selegiline
  • Rasagiline

COMT Inhibitors

  • Entacapone
  • Tolcapone

Other Drugs

  • Amantadine
  • Benztropine
High-Yield: Levodopa can cause dyskinesias and motor fluctuations with long-term use. Dopamine agonists may cause impulse-control problems and excessive sleepiness.

7. Antipsychotic Drugs

Antipsychotic drugs are primarily used in schizophrenia and other psychotic disorders.

Typical Antipsychotics

  • Haloperidol
  • Chlorpromazine
  • Fluphenazine

Typical antipsychotics mainly block dopamine D2 receptors.

Atypical Antipsychotics

  • Clozapine
  • Risperidone
  • Olanzapine
  • Quetiapine
  • Aripiprazole

Most atypical antipsychotics block serotonin 5-HT2A and dopamine D2 receptors. Aripiprazole is a partial agonist at D2 and 5-HT1A receptors.

Extrapyramidal Symptoms

  • Acute dystonia
  • Akathisia
  • Parkinsonism
  • Tardive dyskinesia
Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome: A rare but serious reaction characterized by severe rigidity, hyperthermia, autonomic instability and altered mental status.

Clozapine

Clozapine can be highly effective in treatment-resistant schizophrenia but has a risk of severe neutropenia/agranulocytosis and requires appropriate blood-count monitoring.

8. Antidepressant Drugs

SSRIs

  • Fluoxetine
  • Sertraline
  • Escitalopram
  • Paroxetine

SSRIs inhibit serotonin reuptake and increase serotonin signaling. They are commonly used as first-line treatments for many depressive and anxiety disorders.

SNRIs

  • Venlafaxine
  • Duloxetine

SNRIs increase serotonin and norepinephrine signaling by inhibiting their reuptake.

TCAs

  • Amitriptyline
  • Imipramine
  • Nortriptyline

TCAs inhibit serotonin and norepinephrine reuptake but also block muscarinic, histamine and alpha-adrenergic receptors.

MAO Inhibitors

  • Phenelzine
  • Tranylcypromine
Serotonin Syndrome: May occur with excessive serotonergic activity and can cause agitation, hyperreflexia, clonus, diaphoresis, fever and autonomic instability.

9. Mood Stabilizers

Lithium

Lithium is an important mood stabilizer used in bipolar disorder.

Lithium Toxicity: Tremor, gastrointestinal symptoms, confusion and at higher levels, neurological toxicity.

Long-term lithium therapy can affect thyroid and renal function and may cause nephrogenic diabetes insipidus.

Other Mood Stabilizers

  • Valproate
  • Carbamazepine
  • Lamotrigine

10. Opioid Analgesics

Opioids are powerful analgesic drugs that act primarily at μ-opioid receptors.

Common Opioids

  • Morphine
  • Fentanyl
  • Oxycodone
  • Tramadol
  • Codeine

Major Effects

  • Analgesia
  • Sedation
  • Respiratory depression
  • Miosis
  • Constipation
  • Euphoria
Classic Opioid Toxicity: Respiratory depression + coma + pinpoint pupils.

Opioid Antagonist

Naloxone is a rapidly acting opioid antagonist used to reverse opioid-induced respiratory depression.

Longer-Acting Treatment

Methadone and buprenorphine are used in opioid use disorder treatment in appropriate clinical settings.

11. CNS Stimulants

  • Methylphenidate
  • Amphetamine-based stimulants
  • Caffeine

CNS stimulants increase alertness and reduce fatigue. Methylphenidate and amphetamine-based medicines are used in ADHD.

Adverse Effects: Insomnia, decreased appetite, increased heart rate and blood pressure, and possible anxiety or agitation.

12. Drugs for Alzheimer's Disease

Acetylcholinesterase Inhibitors

  • Donepezil
  • Rivastigmine
  • Galantamine

These drugs increase acetylcholine availability in the CNS and may provide symptomatic benefit in Alzheimer's disease.

NMDA Receptor Antagonist

Memantine is an NMDA receptor antagonist used in moderate to severe Alzheimer's disease.

13. Drugs for Migraine

Acute Treatment

  • NSAIDs
  • Paracetamol
  • Triptans

Triptans

Sumatriptan is a 5-HT1B/1D receptor agonist and causes cranial vasoconstriction and inhibition of trigeminal neuropeptide release.

Preventive Treatment

  • Propranolol
  • Topiramate
  • Valproate
  • Amitriptyline
Important: Triptans are generally avoided in patients with significant ischemic cardiovascular or cerebrovascular disease because of their vasoconstrictive effects.

15. Quick Revision Points

  • GABA is the major inhibitory neurotransmitter in the CNS.
  • Glutamate is the major excitatory neurotransmitter.
  • Benzodiazepines increase the frequency of GABA-A chloride channel opening.
  • Barbiturates increase the duration of GABA-A chloride channel opening.
  • Phenytoin blocks voltage-gated sodium channels.
  • Ethosuximide is especially useful for absence seizures.
  • Levodopa crosses the blood-brain barrier and is converted to dopamine.
  • Carbidopa reduces peripheral conversion of levodopa.
  • Typical antipsychotics mainly block D2 receptors.
  • Clozapine has a risk of severe neutropenia/agranulocytosis.
  • SSRIs inhibit serotonin reuptake.
  • Opioids can cause respiratory depression and miosis.
  • Naloxone reverses opioid-induced respiratory depression.
  • Donepezil inhibits acetylcholinesterase.
  • Memantine is an NMDA receptor antagonist.
  • Sumatriptan is a 5-HT1B/1D agonist.

16. CNS Pharmacology MCQs

1. What is the major inhibitory neurotransmitter in the CNS?

A. Glutamate

B. GABA

C. Dopamine

D. Serotonin

Answer: B. GABA
2. Benzodiazepines increase:

A. Duration of Cl⁻ channel opening

B. Frequency of Cl⁻ channel opening

C. Na⁺ influx

D. Ca²⁺ influx

Answer: B. Frequency of Cl⁻ channel opening
3. Which drug is particularly useful for absence seizures?

A. Ethosuximide

B. Morphine

C. Haloperidol

D. Levodopa

Answer: A. Ethosuximide
4. Which drug is combined with levodopa to reduce peripheral conversion?

A. Naloxone

B. Carbidopa

C. Memantine

D. Flumazenil

Answer: B. Carbidopa
5. Which drug is associated with agranulocytosis/neutropenia risk?

A. Clozapine

B. Fluoxetine

C. Diazepam

D. Morphine

Answer: A. Clozapine
6. Which drug reverses opioid overdose?

A. Flumazenil

B. Naloxone

C. Atropine

D. Pralidoxime

Answer: B. Naloxone
7. Which drug is an NMDA receptor antagonist?

A. Memantine

B. Donepezil

C. Levodopa

D. Phenytoin

Answer: A. Memantine
8. Which drug is an SSRI?

A. Fluoxetine

B. Haloperidol

C. Lithium

D. Phenytoin

Answer: A. Fluoxetine
9. Sumatriptan primarily acts on:

A. GABA-A receptors

B. 5-HT1B/1D receptors

C. D2 receptors

D. NMDA receptors

Answer: B. 5-HT1B/1D receptors
10. Which drug is a benzodiazepine receptor antagonist?

A. Flumazenil

B. Naloxone

C. Memantine

D. Carbidopa

Answer: A. Flumazenil
