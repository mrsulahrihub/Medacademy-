🫃 Abdomen Anatomy

Important structures, organs and exam points

📚 Introduction

The abdomen is the region of the body located between the thorax and pelvis. It contains important organs of the digestive, urinary and vascular systems.

Main abdominal organs:

  • Stomach
  • Liver
  • Gallbladder
  • Spleen
  • Pancreas
  • Small intestine
  • Large intestine
  • Kidneys
  • Adrenal glands

🧱 Abdominal Wall

The abdominal wall protects the abdominal organs and helps in movements of the trunk and respiration.

Important muscles:

  • External oblique
  • Internal oblique
  • Transversus abdominis
  • Rectus abdominis

🫧 Peritoneum

The peritoneum is a serous membrane associated with the abdominal cavity and its organs.

  • Parietal peritoneum lines the abdominal wall.
  • Visceral peritoneum covers many abdominal organs.
  • The peritoneal cavity lies between these layers.

🍽️ Stomach

The stomach is a muscular organ of the digestive system. It lies mainly in the upper left part of the abdomen.

Main parts:

  • Cardia
  • Fundus
  • Body
  • Pyloric part

Important functions:

  • Stores food
  • Mixes food
  • Begins protein digestion
  • Passes chyme into the duodenum

🟤 Liver

The liver is the largest internal organ and is located mainly in the right upper quadrant of the abdomen.

Important functions:

  • Produces bile
  • Metabolizes nutrients
  • Stores glycogen
  • Detoxifies many substances
  • Synthesizes important plasma proteins

Main lobes:

  • Right lobe
  • Left lobe
  • Caudate lobe
  • Quadrate lobe

🟢 Gallbladder

The gallbladder is a small pear-shaped organ located on the inferior surface of the liver.

Functions:

  • Stores bile
  • Concentrates bile
  • Releases bile into the biliary tract

🟣 Spleen

The spleen is located mainly in the left upper quadrant of the abdomen, near the stomach.

Functions:

  • Participates in immune responses
  • Filters blood
  • Removes aged red blood cells
  • Stores some blood cells and platelets

🟡 Pancreas

The pancreas is an elongated gland located posterior to the stomach.

Parts:

  • Head
  • Neck
  • Body
  • Tail

Functions:

  • Produces digestive enzymes
  • Produces bicarbonate
  • Produces hormones such as insulin and glucagon

🧬 Small Intestine

The small intestine is the major site for digestion and absorption of nutrients.

Three parts:

  • Duodenum
  • Jejunum
  • Ileum

🧬 Large Intestine

The large intestine absorbs water and electrolytes and forms and stores fecal material.

Main parts:

  • Caecum
  • Appendix
  • Ascending colon
  • Transverse colon
  • Descending colon
  • Sigmoid colon
  • Rectum

🫘 Kidneys

The kidneys are paired retroperitoneal organs located on the posterior abdominal wall.

Main functions:

  • Filter blood
  • Produce urine
  • Regulate fluid balance
  • Help regulate electrolytes
  • Contribute to blood pressure regulation

🟠 Adrenal Glands

The adrenal glands are located superior to the kidneys.

Two major regions:

  • Adrenal cortex
  • Adrenal medulla

They produce hormones involved in metabolism, stress responses and regulation of salt and water balance.

🩸 Major Blood Vessels

Abdominal Aorta

The abdominal aorta supplies blood to the abdominal organs and posterior abdominal wall through its branches.

Inferior Vena Cava

The inferior vena cava returns venous blood from the lower part of the body to the heart.

Portal Vein

The hepatic portal vein carries nutrient-rich venous blood from much of the gastrointestinal tract to the liver.

⭐ Important Exam Points

  • Stomach is mainly located in the upper left abdomen.
  • Liver is mainly located in the right upper abdomen.
  • Spleen is located in the left upper quadrant.
  • Pancreas lies posterior to the stomach.
  • Small intestine consists of duodenum, jejunum and ileum.
  • Large intestine includes the caecum and colon.
  • Kidneys are retroperitoneal organs.
  • Gallbladder stores and concentrates bile.
  • Portal vein carries blood to the liver from much of the GI tract.

⚡ Quick Revision

  • Stomach: Digestion and food storage
  • Liver: Metabolism, bile production and detoxification
  • Gallbladder: Stores bile
  • Spleen: Blood filtration and immune function
  • Pancreas: Digestive enzymes and hormones
  • Small intestine: Major nutrient absorption
  • Large intestine: Water absorption and feces formation
  • Kidneys: Urine formation and fluid regulation
  • Adrenal glands: Hormone production
