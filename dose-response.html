1. Introduction to Dose-Response

The dose-response relationship describes the relationship between the amount or concentration of a drug and the biological response produced by that drug.

Key Concept: As the dose of a drug increases, its effect generally increases until a maximum response is reached.

2. Important Terms

Dose

Dose is the quantity of a drug administered to a patient.

Response

Response is the measurable physiological or biochemical effect produced by a drug.

Concentration

Drug concentration refers to the amount of drug present in a particular volume, often measured at the site of action or in plasma.

3. Types of Dose-Response Relationships

Two major types of dose-response relationships are commonly discussed in pharmacology.

Type Description
Graded dose-response Measures the magnitude of response in an individual biological system.
Quantal dose-response Measures an all-or-none response in a population.

4. Graded Dose-Response Relationship

A graded response varies continuously with increasing drug concentration or dose.

Characteristics

  • Usually studied in an individual tissue or preparation.
  • Response increases progressively with dose.
  • Eventually reaches a maximum response.
  • Helps determine potency and efficacy.
Example: Increasing concentrations of a bronchodilator may produce progressively greater relaxation of airway smooth muscle until maximum relaxation is reached.

5. Quantal Dose-Response Relationship

A quantal response is an all-or-none response measured across a population.

Examples include whether a patient experiences a particular therapeutic effect or whether a predefined toxic effect occurs.

Uses

  • Determining ED50
  • Determining TD50
  • Determining LD50 in experimental settings
  • Assessing variability of drug responses in a population

6. Emax

Emax represents the maximum effect that a drug can produce in a particular system.

Emax is related to drug efficacy.

Once Emax has been reached, increasing the dose generally does not increase the desired effect further, although toxicity may continue to increase.

7. EC50

EC50 is the concentration of a drug that produces 50% of its maximum effect.

EC50 = Concentration producing 50% of Emax
Exam Point: A lower EC50 generally indicates greater potency.

8. ED50

ED50 is the dose that produces the specified therapeutic effect in 50% of a population.

ED50 is commonly used when discussing quantal dose-response relationships.

9. TD50

TD50 is the dose that produces a specified toxic effect in 50% of a population.

TD50 is used in calculations of the therapeutic index.

10. Potency

Potency describes the amount of drug required to produce a particular effect.

Lower EC50 → Greater Potency

Potency should not be confused with efficacy.

11. Efficacy

Efficacy refers to the maximum effect that a drug can produce.

Emax → Measure of Maximum Drug Effect
A drug can be more potent but less efficacious than another drug.

12. Potency vs Efficacy

Feature Potency Efficacy
Meaning Amount required to produce an effect Maximum effect produced
Common measure EC50 Emax
Main question How much drug is needed? How much effect can it produce?

13. Log Dose-Response Curve

Dose-response data are often plotted against the logarithm of drug concentration or dose.

This commonly produces a sigmoid-shaped curve over the useful concentration range.

The log scale allows a wide range of drug concentrations to be displayed more conveniently.

14. Effect of Competitive Antagonists

A reversible competitive antagonist competes with an agonist for the same receptor site.

Increasing the agonist concentration can overcome the antagonistic effect.

Typical curve change: A reversible competitive antagonist causes a rightward shift of the agonist concentration-response curve without reducing the maximum response when sufficient agonist concentration can overcome the antagonism.

15. Therapeutic Index

Therapeutic index provides an estimate of the separation between doses producing therapeutic and toxic effects.

Therapeutic Index = TD50 / ED50
A larger therapeutic index generally indicates a wider separation between therapeutic and toxic doses.

16. Therapeutic Window

The therapeutic window is the range of drug concentrations or doses between the minimum effective concentration and the concentration at which unacceptable toxicity begins.

Drugs with a narrow therapeutic window require careful dosing and may require therapeutic drug monitoring.

17. Spare Receptors

Spare receptors exist when a maximal response can be achieved without occupying all available receptors.

The presence of spare receptors can increase the sensitivity of a tissue to an agonist.

18. Important Dose-Response Terms

Term Meaning
Emax Maximum effect produced by a drug
EC50 Concentration producing 50% of maximum effect
ED50 Dose producing a specified effect in 50% of a population
TD50 Dose producing a specified toxic effect in 50% of a population
LD50 Dose causing death in 50% of an experimental population

19. Quick Revision Points

  • Dose-response describes the relationship between drug dose/concentration and biological response.
  • Graded response measures response magnitude in an individual biological system.
  • Quantal response measures an all-or-none outcome in a population.
  • Emax is associated with maximum efficacy.
  • EC50 is associated with potency.
  • Lower EC50 generally means greater potency.
  • ED50 is the dose producing a specified effect in 50% of a population.
  • TD50 is the dose producing a specified toxic effect in 50% of a population.
  • Therapeutic Index = TD50 / ED50.
  • A reversible competitive antagonist typically shifts the agonist curve to the right without reducing Emax.

20. Important MCQs

Q1. Emax primarily represents:

A. Drug absorption
B. Drug potency
C. Maximum drug effect
D. Drug elimination

Answer: C. Maximum drug effect
Q2. EC50 is mainly used as a measure of:

A. Potency
B. Toxicity
C. Bioavailability
D. Clearance

Answer: A. Potency
Q3. A graded dose-response curve measures:

A. All-or-none response in a population
B. Magnitude of response in an individual system
C. Drug metabolism
D. Drug excretion

Answer: B. Magnitude of response in an individual system
Q4. Therapeutic Index is calculated as:

A. ED50 / TD50
B. TD50 / ED50
C. EC50 / Emax
D. Emax / EC50

Answer: B. TD50 / ED50
Q5. A lower EC50 generally indicates:

A. Lower potency
B. Greater potency
C. Lower efficacy
D. Greater toxicity

Answer: B. Greater potency
Q6. A reversible competitive antagonist usually causes:

A. Leftward shift of the agonist curve
B. Rightward shift without reducing Emax
C. Complete loss of response
D. Increased Emax

Answer: B. Rightward shift without reducing Emax
