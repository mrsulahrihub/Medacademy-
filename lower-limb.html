🦵 Lower Limb Anatomy

Complete high-yield notes for medical students.

📖 Introduction

The lower limb is designed mainly for weight bearing, posture and locomotion. It includes the hip, thigh, leg and foot.

The major regions are the gluteal region, thigh, knee, leg, ankle and foot.

🦴 1. Bones of Lower Limb

The bones of the lower limb provide support and allow movement during standing and walking.

  • Hip bone
  • Femur
  • Patella
  • Tibia
  • Fibula
  • Tarsal bones
  • Metatarsal bones
  • Phalanges

Important Bones

Bone Important Point
Femur The longest and strongest bone of the human body.
Patella Sesamoid bone located within the quadriceps tendon.
Tibia Main weight-bearing bone of the leg.
Fibula Lateral bone of the leg and important for ankle stability.

💪 2. Muscles of Lower Limb

The muscles of the lower limb are responsible for movement of the hip, knee, ankle and foot.

Major Muscles

  • Gluteus maximus
  • Gluteus medius
  • Quadriceps femoris
  • Hamstrings
  • Gastrocnemius
  • Soleus
  • Tibialis anterior

Important Functions

Muscle / Group Main Action
Gluteus maximus Powerful extensor of the hip.
Gluteus medius Abducts the thigh and helps stabilize the pelvis.
Quadriceps Main extensor group of the knee.
Hamstrings Flex the knee and extend the hip.
Gastrocnemius Important plantar flexor of the ankle.
Tibialis anterior Mainly dorsiflexes the ankle.

🧠 3. Nerves of Lower Limb

The major nerves of the lower limb arise from the lumbar and sacral plexuses.

  • Femoral nerve
  • Obturator nerve
  • Sciatic nerve
  • Tibial nerve
  • Common fibular (peroneal) nerve

Important Nerve Associations

Nerve Important Association
Femoral nerve Major nerve of the anterior thigh.
Obturator nerve Important nerve of the medial thigh.
Sciatic nerve Major nerve supplying the posterior thigh and its branches supply much of the leg and foot.
Tibial nerve Major nerve of the posterior leg.
Common fibular nerve Supplies branches to the anterior and lateral compartments of the leg.

🩸 4. Arteries

The main arterial supply of the lower limb is derived from the external iliac artery.

  • External iliac artery
  • Femoral artery
  • Popliteal artery
  • Anterior tibial artery
  • Posterior tibial artery

Basic Arterial Sequence

External iliac artery → Femoral artery → Popliteal artery → Anterior and Posterior Tibial arteries

🩸 5. Veins

The lower limb contains superficial and deep venous systems.

  • Great saphenous vein
  • Small saphenous vein
  • Deep veins accompanying the arteries

Clinical Importance

The great saphenous vein is an important superficial vein of the lower limb and is commonly discussed in clinical anatomy.

🦵 6. Major Joints

Joint Important Feature
Hip joint Connects the femur with the acetabulum of the hip bone.
Knee joint Major weight-bearing joint between the thigh and leg.
Ankle joint Connects the leg with the foot and permits important foot movements.

⭐ Important Exam Points

  • Femur is the longest and strongest bone of the body.
  • Tibia is the main weight-bearing bone of the leg.
  • Quadriceps is the major extensor group of the knee.
  • Hamstrings help flex the knee and extend the hip.
  • Sciatic nerve is the major nerve of the lower limb.
  • Femoral artery is the main artery of the thigh.
  • Great saphenous vein is an important superficial vein.

⚡ Quick Revision

  • Main bones → Hip bone, Femur, Patella, Tibia, Fibula, Tarsals, Metatarsals, Phalanges
  • Major muscles → Gluteus maximus, Quadriceps, Hamstrings, Gastrocnemius, Soleus
  • Major nerves → Femoral, Obturator, Sciatic, Tibial, Common fibular
  • Main arterial sequence → External iliac → Femoral → Popliteal → Tibial arteries
  • Important superficial veins → Great saphenous, Small saphenous
  • Major joints → Hip, Knee, Ankle
