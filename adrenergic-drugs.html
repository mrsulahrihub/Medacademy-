Adrenergic Drugs

MedAcademy - Medical Students

1. Introduction to Adrenergic Drugs

Adrenergic drugs are medications that mimic, enhance, or block the effects of the sympathetic nervous system.

The major endogenous catecholamines are:

  • Adrenaline (Epinephrine)
  • Noradrenaline (Norepinephrine)
  • Dopamine

These drugs act mainly on alpha (α) and beta (β) adrenergic receptors.

High-Yield Classification:
  • Adrenergic agonists / sympathomimetics
  • Alpha-adrenergic agonists
  • Beta-adrenergic agonists
  • Mixed α/β agonists
  • Alpha blockers
  • Beta blockers
  • Mixed alpha and beta blockers

2. Adrenergic Receptors

Receptor G-Protein Main Effects
α1 Gq Vasoconstriction, mydriasis, increased sphincter tone
α2 Gi ↓ Norepinephrine release, ↓ sympathetic outflow
β1 Gs ↑ Heart rate, ↑ contractility, ↑ renin release
β2 Gs Bronchodilation, vasodilation, uterine relaxation
β3 Gs Detrusor relaxation, lipolysis
Memory Trick: α1 = Gq α2 = Gi β1, β2, β3 = Gs

3. α1 Receptors

α1 receptors are mainly located postsynaptically on vascular smooth muscle and several other tissues.

Major Effects

  • Vasoconstriction
  • ↑ Peripheral vascular resistance
  • ↑ Blood pressure
  • Mydriasis without cycloplegia
  • Contraction of bladder neck and prostate smooth muscle
  • Contraction of gastrointestinal sphincters

Important α1 Agonist

Phenylephrine is a relatively selective α1 agonist.

4. α2 Receptors

α2 receptors are often located presynaptically and reduce neurotransmitter release by decreasing intracellular cAMP.

Major Effects

  • ↓ Norepinephrine release
  • ↓ Sympathetic outflow from the CNS
  • Reduction in blood pressure with centrally acting α2 agonists

Examples

  • Clonidine
  • Methyldopa
  • Dexmedetomidine
High-Yield: α2 activation generally decreases sympathetic activity.

5. β1 Receptors

β1 receptors are especially important in the heart and kidney.

Major Effects

  • ↑ Heart rate (positive chronotropy)
  • ↑ Cardiac contractility (positive inotropy)
  • ↑ AV conduction (positive dromotropy)
  • ↑ Renin release from the kidney
Memory: β1 = 1 heart + 1 kidney

6. β2 Receptors

β2 receptors are important in smooth muscle relaxation.

Major Effects

  • Bronchodilation
  • Vasodilation in selected vascular beds
  • Uterine relaxation
  • Relaxation of some gastrointestinal smooth muscle
  • Promotes potassium uptake into cells
Most important clinical effect: β2 stimulation → Bronchodilation

7. β3 Receptors

β3 receptors are involved in metabolic effects and relaxation of the urinary bladder detrusor muscle.

  • D,etrusor relaxation
  • Promotion of lipolysis

Example

Mirabegron is a β3 agonist used in overactive bladder.

8. Sympathomimetic Drugs

Sympathomimetics are drugs that increase sympathetic activity by directly stimulating adrenergic receptors or indirectly increasing catecholamine activity.

Direct-Acting Sympathomimetics

  • Epinephrine
  • Norepinephrine
  • Phenylephrine
  • Dobutamine
  • Salbutamol (Albuterol)
  • Terbutaline

Indirect-Acting Sympathomimetics

  • Amphetamine
  • Indirectly acting sympathomimetics increase catecholamine availability.

Mixed-Acting Drugs

  • Ephedrine

9. Epinephrine (Adrenaline)

Epinephrine is a potent agonist at both α and β adrenergic receptors. Its effects depend on dose and clinical setting.

Receptor Effects

Receptor Important Effect
α1 Vasoconstriction
β1 ↑ Heart rate and contractility
β2 Bronchodilation

Important Clinical Uses

  • Anaphylaxis
  • Cardiac arrest during resuscitation
  • Severe bronchospasm in selected emergency situations
  • Added to local anesthetics to reduce systemic absorption and prolong action
First-line drug for anaphylaxis: Intramuscular epinephrine.

10. Phenylephrine

Phenylephrine is a relatively selective α1 adrenergic agonist.

Effects

  • Vasoconstriction
  • ↑ Blood pressure
  • Nasal decongestion
  • Mydriasis

Important Adverse Effect

Because of increased blood pressure, reflex bradycardia may occur.

Exam Point: Phenylephrine → α1 agonist → vasoconstriction.

11. Salbutamol (Albuterol)

Salbutamol is a relatively selective β2 adrenergic agonist.

Main Effect

β2 stimulation causes relaxation of bronchial smooth muscle and produces bronchodilation.

Clinical Uses

  • Relief of acute bronchospasm
  • Asthma
  • Other obstructive airway conditions where a short-acting bronchodilator is indicated

Adverse Effects

  • Tremor
  • Tachycardia
  • Palpitations
  • Hypokalemia, especially with higher systemic exposure
High-Yield: Salbutamol → β2 agonist → bronchodilation.

12. Dobutamine

Dobutamine primarily stimulates β1 receptors and increases cardiac contractility.

Clinical Use

  • Short-term management of selected cases of acute cardiac failure
  • Cardiac stress testing in selected patients

Main Effect

↑ Cardiac contractility

13. Norepinephrine (Noradrenaline)

Norepinephrine strongly stimulates α receptors and also stimulates β1 receptors.

Main Effects

  • Marked vasoconstriction
  • ↑ Blood pressure
  • Some increase in cardiac contractility

It is commonly used as a vasopressor in severe hypotension and distributive shock under appropriate monitoring.

14. Alpha-Adrenergic Blockers

Alpha blockers inhibit α adrenergic receptors and reduce the effects of catecholamines at those receptors.

Selective α1 Blockers

  • Prazosin
  • Doxazosin
  • Terazosin
  • Tamsulosin

Nonselective Alpha Blockers

  • Phenoxybenzamine
  • Phentolamine

15. Prazosin

Prazosin is a selective α1 adrenergic receptor antagonist.

Effects

  • Vasodilation
  • ↓ Peripheral vascular resistance
  • ↓ Blood pressure
  • Relaxation of smooth muscle in the lower urinary tract

Clinical Uses

  • Hypertension in selected patients
  • Benign prostatic hyperplasia in selected cases
  • Other selected clinical uses
First-dose effect: Prazosin may cause marked postural hypotension or syncope, particularly after the first dose.

16. Tamsulosin

Tamsulosin preferentially blocks α1 receptors in the prostate and bladder neck.

Main Use

Benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH)

Important Adverse Effects

  • Dizziness
  • Orthostatic hypotension can occur
  • Ejaculatory dysfunction
Tamsulosin improves urinary flow by relaxing smooth muscle in the prostate and bladder neck.

17. Phenoxybenzamine

Phenoxybenzamine is a long-acting, irreversible, nonselective α-adrenergic antagonist.

Important Clinical Use

It is classically used for preoperative preparation of patients with pheochromocytoma.

Important Principle: In pheochromocytoma, adequate α blockade is established before β blockade when both are required.

18. Beta-Adrenergic Blockers

Beta blockers antagonize β adrenergic receptors.

Major Groups

Group Examples
Nonselective β blockers Propranolol, Nadolol, Timolol
β1-selective blockers Metoprolol, Atenolol, Bisoprolol, Esmolol
Mixed α1 + β blockers Carvedilol, Labetalol

19. Propranolol

Propranolol is a nonselective β-adrenergic blocker. It blocks both β1 and β2 receptors.

Clinical Uses

  • Hypertension in selected patients
  • Angina
  • Some cardiac arrhythmias
  • Essential tremor
  • Migraine prevention
  • Hyperthyroid symptom control
  • Portal hypertension in selected settings

Important Adverse Effects

  • Bradycardia
  • Hypotension
  • Fatigue
  • Bronchospasm
  • Can mask adrenergic symptoms of hypoglycemia
Caution: Nonselective beta blockers can worsen bronchospasm in susceptible patients with asthma.

20. β1-Selective Blockers

β1-selective blockers preferentially block β1 receptors at usual therapeutic doses, although selectivity is not absolute.

Examples

  • Metoprolol
  • Atenolol
  • Bisoprolol
  • Esmolol

Effects

  • ↓ Heart rate
  • ↓ Cardiac contractility
  • ↓ AV conduction
  • ↓ Renin release
β1 blockade → ↓ heart rate + ↓ contractility + ↓ renin

21. Carvedilol and Labetalol

Carvedilol

Carvedilol blocks β receptors and also has α1-blocking activity.

It is an important drug in the management of selected patients with heart failure with reduced ejection fraction.

Labetalol

Labetalol blocks both α1 and β receptors. It is commonly used in selected hypertensive emergencies and hypertension associated with pregnancy.

22. Important Clinical Uses of Beta Blockers

  • Hypertension
  • Angina
  • Selected cardiac arrhythmias
  • Post-myocardial infarction therapy
  • Heart failure with selected evidence-based beta blockers
  • Glaucoma
  • Essential tremor
  • Migraine prevention
  • Hyperthyroidism symptom control

Timolol

Timolol is a nonselective beta blocker commonly used topically to reduce intraocular pressure in glaucoma.

23. Adverse Effects of Beta Blockers

  • Bradycardia
  • Hypotension
  • Fatigue
  • Dizziness
  • AV conduction slowing
  • Sexual dysfunction in some patients
  • Bronchospasm with nonselective agents
  • Masking of adrenergic warning symptoms of hypoglycemia
Beta blockers should not usually be stopped abruptly after long-term therapy because rebound sympathetic effects can occur.

24. High-Yield Adrenergic Receptor Summary

Receptor G Protein Key Effect Important Drug
α1 Gq Vasoconstriction Phenylephrine / Prazosin
α2 Gi ↓ Sympathetic outflow Clonidine
β1 Gs ↑ Heart activity, ↑ renin Dobutamine / Metoprolol
β2 Gs Bronchodilation Salbutamol
β3 Gs Detrusor relaxation Mirabegron

25. Agonists vs Antagonists

Drug Action Main Clinical Importance
Epinephrine α + β agonist Anaphylaxis, cardiac arrest
Phenylephrine α1 agonist Vasoconstriction, decongestion
Salbutamol β2 agonist Bronchodilation
Dobutamine Predominantly β1 agonist ↑ Cardiac contractility
Prazosin α1 blocker Hypertension, selected BPH use
Tamsulosin Preferential α1 blocker BPH
Propranolol β1 + β2 blocker Multiple cardiovascular and non-cardiovascular uses
Metoprolol β1-selective blocker Cardiovascular disease
Carvedilol α1 + β blocker Heart failure

26. High-Yield Exam Points

  • α1 → Gq → vasoconstriction.
  • α2 → Gi → ↓ norepinephrine release and sympathetic outflow.
  • β1 → Gs → ↑ heart rate, contractility and renin.
  • β2 → Gs → bronchodilation.
  • β3 → Gs → detrusor relaxation.
  • Epinephrine stimulates α and β receptors.
  • Phenylephrine is mainly an α1 agonist.
  • Salbutamol is a β2 agonist.
  • Dobutamine mainly increases cardiac contractility through β1 stimulation.
  • Prazosin is an α1 blocker.
  • Tamsulosin is used mainly for BPH.
  • Phenoxybenzamine is a classic drug for preoperative α blockade in pheochromocytoma.
  • Propranolol is a nonselective beta blocker.
  • Metoprolol is relatively β1-selective.
  • Carvedilol blocks α1 and β receptors.
  • Labetalol blocks α1 and β receptors.
  • Nonselective beta blockers can cause bronchospasm.
  • In pheochromocytoma, establish adequate α blockade before β blockade if both are needed.

27. Important MCQs

Q1. Which G-protein is associated with α1 receptors?

  1. Gi
  2. Gs
  3. Gq
  4. G12

Answer: C — Gq

Q2. Which receptor is mainly responsible for bronchodilation?

  1. α1
  2. α2
  3. β1
  4. β2

Answer: D — β2

Q3. Which drug is a relatively selective α1 agonist?

  1. Salbutamol
  2. Phenylephrine
  3. Propranolol
  4. Metoprolol

Answer: B — Phenylephrine

Q4. Which drug is commonly used for acute bronchospasm?

  1. Salbutamol
  2. Prazosin
  3. Propranolol
  4. Phenoxybenzamine

Answer: A — Salbutamol

Q5. Which drug is first-line for anaphylaxis?

  1. Atropine
  2. Phenylephrine
  3. Epinephrine
  4. Propranolol

Answer: C — Epinephrine

Q6. Which receptor increases renin release?

  1. α1
  2. α2
  3. β1
  4. β2

Answer: C — β1

Q7. Which drug is an α1 blocker?

  1. Prazosin
  2. Phenylephrine
  3. Salbutamol
  4. Dobutamine

Answer: A — Prazosin

Q8. Which drug is particularly associated with treatment of BPH?

  1. Tamsulosin
  2. Epinephrine
  3. Phenylephrine
  4. Dobutamine

Answer: A — Tamsulosin

Q9. Which is a nonselective beta blocker?

  1. Metoprolol
  2. Propranolol
  3. Esmolol
  4. Bisoprolol

Answer: B — Propranolol

Q10. Which beta blocker also has α1-blocking activity?

  1. Propranolol
  2. Metoprolol
  3. Carvedilol
  4. Atenolol

Answer: C — Carvedilol

Q11. Which drug is classically used for preoperative α blockade in pheochromocytoma?

  1. Phenoxybenzamine
  2. Salbutamol
  3. Metoprolol
  4. Dobutamine

Answer: A — Phenoxybenzamine

Q12. What is the important receptor effect of dobutamine?

  1. ↓ Cardiac contractility
  2. ↑ Cardiac contractility
  3. Bronchoconstriction
  4. α1 blockade

Answer: B — ↑ Cardiac contractility

Q13. Which beta blocker may worsen bronchospasm because it blocks β2 receptors?

  1. Propranolol
  2. Metoprolol
  3. Bisoprolol
  4. Esmolol

Answer: A — Propranolol

Q14. Which receptor is coupled to Gi?

  1. α1
  2. α2
  3. β1
  4. β2

Answer: B — α2

Q15. Which drug is a β3 agonist?

  1. Mirabegron
  2. Phenylephrine
  3. Prazosin
  4. Propranolol

Answer: A — Mirabegron
