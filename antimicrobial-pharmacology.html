1. Introduction to Antimicrobial Pharmacology

Antimicrobial pharmacology deals with drugs used to prevent and treat infections caused by bacteria, viruses, fungi and parasites.

Major Antimicrobial Groups:
  • Antibacterial drugs
  • Antiviral drugs
  • Antifungal drugs
  • Antiprotozoal drugs
  • Anthelmintic drugs

Antimicrobial therapy aims to eliminate or inhibit microorganisms while causing minimal harm to the patient.

2. Major Targets of Antibacterial Drugs

Target Important Drug Classes
Cell Wall Synthesis Penicillins, Cephalosporins, Carbapenems, Vancomycin
Protein Synthesis - 30S Aminoglycosides, Tetracyclines
Protein Synthesis - 50S Macrolides, Clindamycin, Linezolid
DNA/RNA Synthesis Fluoroquinolones, Rifampicin, Metronidazole
Folate Synthesis Sulfonamides, Trimethoprim
Cell Membrane Daptomycin

3. Penicillins

Penicillins are beta-lactam antibiotics that inhibit bacterial cell wall synthesis by binding penicillin-binding proteins and interfering with peptidoglycan cross-linking.

Examples

  • Penicillin G
  • Penicillin V
  • Amoxicillin
  • Ampicillin
  • Oxacillin
  • Nafcillin
  • Piperacillin

Important Uses

  • Streptococcal infections
  • Syphilis - penicillin G
  • Enterococcal infections - selected regimens
  • Respiratory infections - selected agents
  • Pseudomonas - piperacillin/tazobactam
Major Adverse Effect: Hypersensitivity reactions including rash and, rarely, anaphylaxis.

4. Cephalosporins

Cephalosporins are beta-lactam antibiotics that inhibit bacterial cell wall synthesis.

Generation Examples Important Features
1st Cefazolin, Cephalexin Good Gram-positive coverage
2nd Cefuroxime, Cefoxitin Increased Gram-negative coverage
3rd Ceftriaxone, Cefotaxime, Ceftazidime Strong Gram-negative activity
4th Cefepime Broad coverage including Pseudomonas
5th Ceftaroline Activity against MRSA
High-Yield: Ceftriaxone is widely used for serious infections and is an important drug in the treatment of bacterial meningitis and gonorrhea.
