🧬 Cell Physiology

Cell membrane, transport mechanisms and membrane potentials

📚 Introduction

Cell physiology deals with the normal functions of cells and how cells maintain their internal environment.

The cell membrane plays a major role in controlling the movement of substances into and out of the cell.

🧱 Cell Membrane

The cell membrane is a selectively permeable membrane surrounding the cell.

Major components:

  • Phospholipids
  • Membrane proteins
  • Cholesterol
  • Carbohydrate-containing molecules

Important functions:

  • Separates intracellular and extracellular environments
  • Controls movement of substances
  • Receives signals through receptors
  • Helps maintain membrane potential
  • Participates in cell communication

💧 Diffusion

Diffusion is the passive movement of particles from an area of higher concentration toward an area of lower concentration.

Important points:

  • Does not directly require cellular energy (ATP).
  • Movement occurs down a concentration gradient.
  • Small lipid-soluble molecules can cross the membrane by simple diffusion.

🚪 Facilitated Diffusion

Facilitated diffusion is passive transport through specific membrane proteins.

Features:

  • Does not directly use ATP.
  • Uses carrier proteins or channels.
  • Moves substances down their electrochemical gradient.
  • Can be selective and saturable.

💦 Osmosis

Osmosis is the movement of water across a selectively permeable membrane toward the side with higher effective solute concentration.

Important terms:

  • Hypotonic: Lower effective osmolarity than the reference solution.
  • Isotonic: Similar effective osmolarity.
  • Hypertonic: Higher effective osmolarity.

⚡ Active Transport

Active transport moves substances against their electrochemical gradient and requires energy.

Primary active transport:

Uses ATP directly to transport ions or other substances.

Example:

  • Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase

Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase:

  • Moves 3 Na⁺ out of the cell.
  • Moves 2 K⁺ into the cell.
  • Uses ATP.
  • Helps maintain Na⁺ and K⁺ gradients.

🔄 Secondary Active Transport

Secondary active transport uses the energy stored in an ion gradient, usually the Na⁺ gradient, rather than directly hydrolyzing ATP at the transporter.

Two common types:

  • Cotransport (symport): substances move in the same direction.
  • Countertransport (antiport): substances move in opposite directions.

📦 Endocytosis & Exocytosis

Endocytosis

Endocytosis brings substances into the cell by forming membrane vesicles.

Types include:

  • Phagocytosis
  • Pinocytosis
  • Receptor-mediated endocytosis

Exocytosis

Exocytosis releases substances from the cell when intracellular vesicles fuse with the plasma membrane.

⚡ Resting Membrane Potential

Resting membrane potential is the electrical potential difference across the cell membrane when the cell is at rest.

In many neurons, the resting membrane potential is approximately -70 mV, although the exact value varies by cell type.

Major contributing factors:

  • Unequal distribution of ions across the membrane
  • Selective permeability of the membrane
  • K⁺ leak channels
  • Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase

⚡ Action Potential

An action potential is a rapid, temporary change in membrane potential that allows certain excitable cells to transmit signals.

Main phases:

  1. Resting state
  2. Depolarization
  3. Repolarization
  4. After-hyperpolarization
  5. Return toward resting potential

Depolarization:

In a typical neuronal action potential, voltage-gated Na⁺ channels open rapidly, allowing Na⁺ influx.

Repolarization:

Voltage-gated Na⁺ channels inactivate and voltage-gated K⁺ channels contribute to K⁺ efflux, moving the membrane potential back toward negative values.

🚦 Threshold

Threshold is the membrane potential at which an adequate depolarizing stimulus triggers an action potential in an excitable cell.

Once threshold is reached, the action potential follows the all-or-none principle.

⏱️ Refractory Period

The refractory period is the period after an action potential during which another action potential is difficult or impossible to generate.

Two phases:

  • Absolute refractory period: another action potential cannot normally be triggered.
  • Relative refractory period: a stronger-than-usual stimulus may trigger another action potential.

🔌 Ion Channels

Ion channels are membrane proteins that allow specific ions to cross the membrane.

Important types:

  • Leak channels
  • Voltage-gated channels
  • Ligand-gated channels
  • Mechanically gated channels

🧪 Electrochemical Gradient

The electrochemical gradient is determined by both the concentration gradient and the electrical gradient acting on an ion.

Ion movement across a membrane depends on these forces and the membrane's permeability to that ion.

⭐ Important Exam Points

  • Cell membrane is selectively permeable.
  • Simple diffusion is passive and moves down a gradient.
  • Facilitated diffusion uses membrane proteins but does not directly use ATP.
  • Osmosis refers to movement of water across a selectively permeable membrane.
  • Primary active transport directly uses ATP.
  • Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase moves 3 Na⁺ out and 2 K⁺ into the cell per ATP cycle.
  • Secondary active transport uses energy stored in an ion gradient.
  • Resting membrane potential is negative inside relative to outside in many cells.
  • Neuronal resting membrane potential is commonly around -70 mV.
  • Depolarization of a typical neuronal action potential involves Na⁺ influx.
  • Repolarization is strongly associated with K⁺ efflux.
  • Action potentials follow the all-or-none principle.
  • Absolute and relative refractory periods limit repeated action potentials.

  • Osmosis: Movement of water across a selectively permeable membrane
  • Primary active transport: Direct ATP use
  • Na⁺/K⁺ pump: 3 Na⁺ out + 2 K⁺ in
  • Resting potential: Negative inside relative to outside
  • Depolarization: Na⁺ influx in a typical neuronal action potential
  • Repolarization: K⁺ efflux contributes to restoring negativity
  • Threshold: Trigger point for an action potential
  • Refractory period: Limits immediate re-excitation
