Cardiovascular Pharmacology

MedAcademy - Medical Students

1. Introduction to Cardiovascular Pharmacology

Cardiovascular pharmacology deals with drugs used to prevent and treat diseases involving the heart, blood vessels, blood pressure and thrombosis.

Major Topics in this Chapter:

Antihypertensives Antianginals Antiarrhythmics Heart Failure Diuretics Anticoagulants Antiplatelets

2. Antihypertensive Drugs

Antihypertensive drugs lower blood pressure and reduce the risk of complications such as stroke, myocardial infarction, heart failure and kidney disease.

Major Classes

  • ACE inhibitors
  • Angiotensin II receptor blockers (ARBs)
  • Calcium-channel blockers
  • Diuretics
  • Beta blockers
  • Alpha blockers
  • Central sympatholytics
  • Direct vasodilators

3. ACE Inhibitors

ACE inhibitors block angiotensin-converting enzyme and reduce formation of angiotensin II.

Mechanism

ACE inhibition ↓ ↓ Angiotensin II ↓ ↓ Vasoconstriction ↓ ↓ Aldosterone secretion ↓ ↓ Blood pressure

Examples

  • Captopril
  • Enalapril
  • Lisinopril
  • Ramipril

Clinical Uses

  • Hypertension
  • Heart failure
  • Post-myocardial infarction
  • Selected patients with chronic kidney disease and albuminuria

Adverse Effects

  • Dry cough
  • Hyperkalemia
  • Hypotension
  • Rise in creatinine in susceptible patients
  • Angioedema
Contraindicated in pregnancy. ACE inhibitors can cause fetal kidney injury and other serious fetal complications.

4. Angiotensin II Receptor Blockers (ARBs)

ARBs block the angiotensin II AT1 receptor.

Examples

  • Losartan
  • Valsartan
  • Irbesartan
  • Telmisartan

Effects

  • Vasodilation
  • Reduced aldosterone effect
  • Reduced blood pressure
ACE inhibitor vs ARB: ACE inhibitors commonly cause cough because they increase bradykinin. ARBs generally cause much less cough because they do not inhibit bradykinin breakdown.
ARBs are also contraindicated in pregnancy.

5. Calcium-Channel Blockers

Calcium-channel blockers reduce calcium entry through L-type calcium channels.

Two Major Groups

Group Examples Main Effects
Dihydropyridines Amlodipine, Nifedipine Vasodilation
Non-dihydropyridines Verapamil, Diltiazem ↓ Heart rate and AV conduction

Amlodipine

  • Long-acting dihydropyridine
  • Used in hypertension and angina
  • May cause ankle edema

Verapamil

  • Strong cardiac effects
  • Decreases AV conduction
  • Used in selected supraventricular arrhythmias
  • May cause constipation
Verapamil and diltiazem can cause bradycardia and AV conduction slowing.

6. Diuretics

Diuretics increase renal sodium and water excretion. They are important in hypertension, edema and heart failure.

Major Classes

Class Example Main Site Important Use
Loop diuretics Furosemide Thick ascending limb Edema, heart failure
Thiazides Hydrochlorothiazide Early distal tubule Hypertension
K-sparing Amiloride Collecting duct Prevent potassium loss
Aldosterone antagonists Spironolactone Collecting duct Heart failure, hyperaldosteronism

7. Loop Diuretics

Loop diuretics inhibit the Na⁺/K⁺/2Cl⁻ cotransporter in the thick ascending limb of the loop of Henle.

Examples

  • Furosemide
  • Bumetanide
  • Torsemide

Clinical Uses

  • Acute pulmonary edema
  • Heart failure with fluid overload
  • Significant edema
  • Hypercalcemia in selected settings

Adverse Effects

  • Hypokalemia
  • Hyponatremia
  • Hypomagnesemia
  • Dehydration
  • Hypotension
  • Ototoxicity with high exposure or rapid administration
Loop diuretics are powerful diuretics.

8. Thiazide Diuretics

Thiazides inhibit the Na⁺/Cl⁻ cotransporter in the early distal convoluted tubule.

Examples

  • Hydrochlorothiazide
  • Chlorthalidone
  • Indapamide

Clinical Use

Thiazide-type diuretics are important first-line options for hypertension in many patients.

Adverse Effects

  • Hypokalemia
  • Hyponatremia
  • Hyperuricemia
  • Hyperglycemia
  • Hypercalcemia
Thiazides increase calcium reabsorption.

9. Potassium-Sparing Diuretics

Examples

  • Amiloride
  • Triamterene
  • Spironolactone
  • Eplerenone

Important Difference

Amiloride and triamterene block epithelial sodium channels.

Spironolactone and eplerenone block mineralocorticoid (aldosterone) receptors.

Adverse Effect

Hyperkalemia

Spironolactone

Spironolactone may cause gynecomastia and menstrual/endocrine effects because of its antiandrogenic activity.

10. Antianginal Drugs

Antianginal drugs reduce myocardial oxygen demand, increase oxygen supply, or both.

Major Classes

  • Nitrates
  • Beta blockers
  • Calcium-channel blockers
  • Ranolazine

11. Nitrates

Examples

  • Nitroglycerin
  • Isosorbide dinitrate
  • Isosorbide mononitrate

Nitrates increase nitric oxide signaling and cause vascular smooth muscle relaxation.

Main Effect

Venodilation → ↓ Venous return → ↓ Preload → ↓ Myocardial oxygen demand

Clinical Uses

  • Acute relief of angina
  • Chronic angina in selected patients
  • Acute pulmonary edema in selected settings

Adverse Effects

  • Headache
  • Flushing
  • Hypotension
  • Reflex tachycardia
Never combine nitrates with PDE-5 inhibitors such as sildenafil. The combination can cause severe hypotension.

12. Beta Blockers in Angina

Beta blockers decrease heart rate and contractility, reducing myocardial oxygen demand.

Examples

  • Metoprolol
  • Atenolol
  • Propranolol

They are particularly useful for prevention of exertional angina and provide important cardiovascular benefits in selected patients.

Beta blockers are not used as the primary immediate treatment for acute coronary vasospasm.

13. Antiarrhythmic Drugs

Antiarrhythmic drugs are used to control abnormal cardiac rhythms.

Vaughan-Williams Classification:
Class Action Examples
Class I Na⁺ channel blockers Quinidine, Lidocaine, Flecainide
Class II Beta blockers Metoprolol, Esmolol
Class III K⁺ channel blockers Amiodarone, Sotalol
Class IV Ca²⁺ channel blockers Verapamil, Diltiazem

14. Class I Antiarrhythmics

Class I drugs block fast sodium channels. They are divided into IA, IB and IC.

Subclass Examples Key Point
IA Quinidine, Procainamide Moderate Na⁺ block + prolonged repolarization
IB Lidocaine, Mexiletine Preferential action in ischemic ventricular tissue
IC Flecainide, Propafenone Strong Na⁺ block
Class IC drugs are generally avoided in patients with significant structural heart disease because of proarrhythmic risk.

15. Class II Antiarrhythmics

Class II drugs are beta blockers.

Examples

  • Metoprolol
  • Esmolol
  • Propranolol

Effects

  • ↓ Heart rate
  • ↓ AV nodal conduction
  • ↓ Sympathetic stimulation

16. Class III Antiarrhythmics

Class III drugs mainly block potassium channels and prolong cardiac repolarization.

Amiodarone

Amiodarone has properties of several antiarrhythmic classes and is a powerful broad-spectrum antiarrhythmic.

Important Adverse Effects

  • Pulmonary toxicity
  • Thyroid dysfunction
  • Liver toxicity
  • Corneal deposits
  • Photosensitivity
  • Skin discoloration
Amiodarone: Think about lung + thyroid + liver + eye + skin toxicity.

17. Class IV Antiarrhythmics

Class IV antiarrhythmics are non-dihydropyridine calcium-channel blockers.

  • Verapamil
  • Diltiazem

Main Effects

  • ↓ AV nodal conduction
  • ↑ AV nodal refractoriness
  • ↓ Heart rate

They are useful for selected supraventricular tachyarrhythmias.

18. Adenosine

Adenosine is an ultra-short-acting drug that transiently slows AV nodal conduction.

Main Clinical Use

Acute termination of certain AV node-dependent supraventricular tachycardias.

Important Features

  • Very short half-life
  • Transient flushing
  • Chest discomfort
  • Dyspnea
  • Transient AV block
Exam Point: Adenosine → very short acting → AV node → SVT.

19. Drugs for Heart Failure

Heart failure treatment depends on the type of heart failure and clinical status.

Important Drug Groups

  • ACE inhibitors / ARBs / ARNI
  • Evidence-based beta blockers
  • Mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists
  • SGLT2 inhibitors
  • Diuretics
  • ARNI: Sacubitril + Valsartan
  • Ivabradine in selected patients
  • Digoxin in selected patients

20. ACE Inhibitors and ARBs in Heart Failure

ACE inhibitors and ARBs reduce harmful neurohormonal activation and decrease afterload.

Examples

  • Enalapril
  • Lisinopril
  • Losartan
  • Valsartan
They can improve outcomes in selected patients with heart failure with reduced ejection fraction.

21. ARNI - Sacubitril/Valsartan

Sacubitril/valsartan combines neprilysin inhibition with AT1 receptor blockade.

Effects

  • Promotes beneficial natriuretic peptide activity
  • Reduces angiotensin II-mediated effects
  • Reduces cardiac remodeling and disease progression in appropriate patients
When switching from an ACE inhibitor to sacubitril/valsartan, an appropriate washout period is required because of angioedema risk.

22. Beta Blockers in Heart Failure

Certain beta blockers improve survival in selected patients with chronic heart failure with reduced ejection fraction.

Important Evidence-Based Examples

  • Carvedilol
  • Metoprolol succinate
  • Bisoprolol
Beta blockers are generally started at low doses in stable patients and titrated gradually.

23. Mineralocorticoid Receptor Antagonists

Examples

  • Spironolactone
  • Eplerenone

Benefits

  • Reduce harmful aldosterone effects
  • Reduce sodium retention
  • Improve outcomes in selected patients with HFrEF

Major Risk

Hyperkalemia

24. SGLT2 Inhibitors in Heart Failure

Examples

  • Dapagliflozin
  • Empagliflozin

SGLT2 inhibitors have important benefits in heart failure, including reduced risk of hospitalization for heart failure and cardiovascular benefit in appropriate patients.

Their heart-failure benefits extend beyond patients with diabetes.

25. Diuretics in Heart Failure

Diuretics mainly provide symptomatic relief by reducing fluid overload.

Examples

  • Furosemide
  • Bumetanide
  • Torsemide
Diuretics are especially useful when heart failure causes pulmonary or peripheral congestion.

26. Digoxin

Digoxin inhibits the Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase and indirectly increases intracellular calcium in cardiac myocytes.

Effects

  • Positive inotropic effect
  • Increased vagal activity
  • Slows AV nodal conduction

Clinical Use

Digoxin may be used in selected patients with heart failure and/or atrial fibrillation for symptom or rate-control purposes.

Toxicity

  • Nausea and vomiting
  • Visual disturbances
  • Bradyarrhythmias
  • Various cardiac arrhythmias
Hypokalemia can increase the risk of digoxin toxicity.

27. Anticoagulants

Anticoagulants reduce formation or extension of blood clots by interfering with the coagulation cascade.

Major Groups

  • Unfractionated heparin
  • Low-molecular-weight heparin
  • Warfarin
  • Direct oral anticoagulants (DOACs)

28. Heparin

Heparin enhances the activity of antithrombin, leading to inhibition of key coagulation factors.

Unfractionated Heparin

  • Rapid onset when given parenterally
  • Often monitored using aPTT depending on protocol
  • Useful when rapid anticoagulation is needed

Important Adverse Effect

Bleeding

Important Complication

Heparin-induced thrombocytopenia (HIT)

HIT is an immune-mediated complication associated with thrombocytopenia and increased risk of thrombosis.

29. Low-Molecular-Weight Heparins

Example

Enoxaparin

LMWHs predominantly inhibit factor Xa through antithrombin.

Uses

  • Venous thromboembolism prevention
  • Treatment of selected VTE cases
  • Acute coronary syndromes in appropriate settings

30. Warfarin

Warfarin inhibits vitamin K epoxide reductase and decreases production of vitamin K-dependent clotting factors.

Factors Affected

  • II
  • VII
  • IX
  • X

It also decreases the anticoagulant proteins C and S.

Monitoring

Warfarin therapy is monitored using PT/INR.

Adverse Effects

  • Bleeding
  • Drug interactions
  • Skin necrosis in rare circumstances related to early protein C reduction
Warfarin is contraindicated in pregnancy because of fetal risks.

31. Direct Oral Anticoagulants (DOACs)

Drug Main Target
Apixaban Factor Xa
Rivaroxaban Factor Xa
Edoxaban Factor Xa
Dabigatran Direct thrombin inhibitor

Advantages

  • Rapid onset
  • Fixed dosing in many situations
  • Generally no routine INR monitoring
Choice of anticoagulant depends on the indication, kidney function, drug interactions, bleeding risk and patient-specific factors.

32. Antiplatelet Drugs

Antiplatelet drugs inhibit platelet activation or aggregation. They are especially important in arterial thrombosis.

Major Groups

  • Aspirin
  • P2Y12 inhibitors
  • Glycoprotein IIb/IIIa inhibitors

33. Aspirin

Aspirin irreversibly inhibits cyclooxygenase enzymes, reducing thromboxane A2 production in platelets.

Main Effect

↓ Platelet aggregation

Clinical Uses

  • Secondary prevention of cardiovascular events in appropriate patients
  • Acute coronary syndrome in appropriate settings

Adverse Effects

  • Gastrointestinal irritation
  • Gastrointestinal bleeding
  • Bleeding
  • Hypersensitivity reactions in susceptible patients

34. P2Y12 Inhibitors

Examples

  • Clopidogrel
  • Prasugrel
  • Ticagrelor

These drugs inhibit ADP-mediated platelet activation through the P2Y12 receptor.

Clinical Uses

  • Acute coronary syndrome
  • After coronary stent placement
  • Selected patients with atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease
Clopidogrel: Important P2Y12 inhibitor and commonly used antiplatelet drug.

35. GP IIb/IIIa Inhibitors

Examples

  • Abciximab
  • Eptifibatide
  • Tirofiban

These drugs inhibit the final common pathway of platelet aggregation by blocking the GP IIb/IIIa receptor.

Main Risk

Bleeding

36. Anticoagulants vs Antiplatelets

Feature Anticoagulants Antiplatelets
Main target Coagulation factors Platelets
Major role Venous thrombosis and cardioembolic prevention Arterial thrombosis prevention
Examples Heparin, Warfarin, Apixaban Aspirin, Clopidogrel
Main adverse effect Bleeding Bleeding

37. High-Yield Cardiovascular Pharmacology

  • ACE inhibitors decrease angiotensin II formation.
  • ACE inhibitors can cause dry cough and angioedema.
  • ARBs block AT1 receptors.
  • ACE inhibitors and ARBs are contraindicated in pregnancy.
  • Amlodipine is a dihydropyridine calcium-channel blocker.
  • Verapamil and diltiazem slow AV nodal conduction.
  • Loop diuretics are powerful diuretics.
  • Thiazides increase calcium reabsorption.
  • Spironolactone can cause hyperkalemia and gynecomastia.
  • Nitrates mainly reduce preload.
  • Nitrates must not be combined with PDE-5 inhibitors.
  • Class I antiarrhythmics block sodium channels.
  • Class II antiarrhythmics are beta blockers.
  • Class III drugs mainly prolong repolarization through potassium-channel blockade.
  • Class IV drugs are verapamil and diltiazem.
  • Adenosine is very short acting and acts mainly at the AV node.
  • Carvedilol, metoprolol succinate and bisoprolol are important beta blockers in HFrEF.
  • Furosemide is useful for fluid overload.
  • Heparin enhances antithrombin activity.
  • Warfarin is monitored using PT/INR.
  • DOACs include factor Xa inhibitors and dabigatran.
  • Aspirin inhibits platelet COX and reduces thromboxane A2.
  • Clopidogrel blocks the P2Y12 receptor.

38. Important MCQs

Q1. Which drug class commonly causes a dry cough?

  1. ARBs
  2. ACE inhibitors
  3. Calcium-channel blockers
  4. Beta blockers

Answer: B — ACE inhibitors

Q2. Which drug is an ARB?

  1. Enalapril
  2. Losartan
  3. Amlodipine
  4. Furosemide

Answer: B — Losartan

Q3. Which calcium-channel blocker mainly causes vasodilation?

  1. Verapamil
  2. Diltiazem
  3. Amlodipine
  4. Digoxin

Answer: C — Amlodipine

Q4. Which diuretic is particularly useful in acute pulmonary edema?

  1. Furosemide
  2. Amiloride
  3. Spironolactone
  4. Hydrochlorothiazide

Answer: A — Furosemide

Q5. Which diuretic can cause hypercalcemia?

  1. Furosemide
  2. Hydrochlorothiazide
  3. Acetazolamide
  4. Mannitol

Answer: B — Hydrochlorothiazide

Q6. Nitrates primarily reduce:

  1. Preload
  2. Afterload only
  3. Heart rate directly
  4. Platelet count

Answer: A — Preload

Q7. Nitrates should not be combined with:

  1. ACE inhibitors
  2. PDE-5 inhibitors
  3. Statins
  4. Antibiotics

Answer: B — PDE-5 inhibitors

Q8. Which class of antiarrhythmics blocks sodium channels?

  1. Class I
  2. Class II
  3. Class III
  4. Class IV

Answer: A — Class I

Q9. Which drug is a Class III antiarrhythmic?

  1. Amiodarone
  2. Metoprolol
  3. Verapamil
  4. Lidocaine

Answer: A — Amiodarone

Q10. Which drug is commonly used for acute termination of AV node-dependent SVT?

  1. Adenosine
  2. Warfarin
  3. Furosemide
  4. Aspirin

Answer: A — Adenosine

Q11. Which beta blocker is an important evidence-based drug in HFrEF?

  1. Carvedilol
  2. Phenylephrine
  3. Verapamil
  4. Propranolol

Answer: A — Carvedilol

Q12. Which drug is a mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist?

  1. Spironolactone
  2. Furosemide
  3. Amlodipine
  4. Enalapril

Answer: A — Spironolactone

Q13. Which anticoagulant is monitored using PT/INR?

  1. Warfarin
  2. Heparin
  3. Enoxaparin
  4. Dabigatran

Answer: A — Warfarin

Q14. Which drug directly inhibits thrombin?

  1. Apixaban
  2. Dabigatran
  3. Warfarin
  4. Clopidogrel

Answer: B — Dabigatran

Q15. Which drug is a factor Xa inhibitor?

  1. Dabigatran
  2. Apixaban
  3. Warfarin
  4. Aspirin

Answer: B — Apixaban

Q16. Aspirin reduces platelet aggregation mainly by inhibiting:

  1. Thrombin
  2. COX
  3. Factor Xa
  4. AT1 receptor

Answer: B — COX

Q17. Clopidogrel blocks which platelet receptor?

  1. β1
  2. AT1
  3. P2Y12
  4. GP Ib

Answer: C — P2Y12

Q18. Which anticoagulant complication is associated with thrombocytopenia and thrombosis?

  1. HIT
  2. Angioedema
  3. Digoxin toxicity
  4. First-dose effect

Answer: A — Heparin-induced thrombocytopenia

Q19. Which drug can cause gynecomastia?

  1. Spironolactone
  2. Furosemide
  3. Amlodipine
  4. Losartan

Answer: A — Spironolactone

Q20. Which drug is associated with pulmonary and thyroid toxicity?

  1. Amiodarone
  2. Adenosine
  3. Verapamil
  4. Lidocaine

Answer: A — Amiodarone
